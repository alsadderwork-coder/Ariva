# Ariva decision log

Product and architecture decisions in date order. Architecture decisions with consequences and alternatives are in [../architecture/adr/README.md](../architecture/adr/README.md). Numbers such as "decision 8" refer to the numbered scope decisions recorded in D1 to D3 and cited by D4 to D6. A later entry overrides an earlier one where they conflict; superseded entries are marked.

## September 2026 (D1 to D3 and the product owner; exact dates not recorded in the sources)

- Build the airport queue management system as a Dalil Tech product, with no current client. AUH is a likely first target; AMAN is not the immigration system at AUH.
- Separate product and codebase from AMAN, integrating through new aggregate-only contracts; hostable in AMAN's cluster or a separate one (decision 15). ADR-0001.
- Two separately sellable modules: the Border module for border authorities and the Airport Operations module for airport operators (decision 1). ADR-0002.
- Penalties are in scope: a handler SLA and penalty engine in v1.
- Officer-level data for border supervisors only (decision 8), and aggregate-only contracts from AMAN (decision 9). The conflict is resolved in D5: officer-level analytics stay in AMAN and never cross into Ariva. ADR-0010.
- Multi-sensor support is treated like e-gates: vendor-agnostic adapters, all ceiling heights (decisions 13 and 14). Hardware is bought on Claude's recommendation; a local integration partner installs and maintains.
- On-prem first. Separate border and airport deployments, with a one-way aggregate feed from border to airport. ADR-0012.
- AODB feeds (SITA, Amadeus and others) are mocked during development rather than waiting for real feed access (decision 11). ADR-0014.
- The developer works roughly half-time on the product until a pilot contract is signed (decision 17).
- Delivery team assumption: one developer using Claude Code, with the architect and product owner part-time.
- The first pilot is the Border module at one AMAN arrivals hall.
- AUH is approached as a component inside SITA's platform.

## 2026-09-28: D4 How It Works (approved)

- Sensors: overhead 3D stereo vision for ceilings from 2 to about 20 m; LiDAR above that, outdoors, in poor light, or where cameras need legal authorisation. Certify one product family of each before the pilot.
- Wi-Fi or BLE probing and CCTV analytics are not used for queue KPIs (CCTV only as a coarse overflow fallback).
- Continuity by overlapping coverage, never by re-identification. Track ids rotate at zone exit and never outlive the operating day. ADR-0011.
- Two wait numbers: realised wait (attributed to the entry interval, final only once the queue clears, used for reports and penalties) and nowcast (queue length and staffed-desk throughput, used for screens and alerts). ADR-0007, ADR-0008.
- Queue model: backlog recursion with Monte Carlo, not Erlang formulas. ADR-0009.
- Desk state is a state machine (Closed, Idle, Serving, Paused, plus Unknown) fed by signals in strict precedence; a passenger walking up is not a signal.
- Passenger screens show the nowcast in 5-minute bands with hysteresis, never a realised number.
- Validation protocol per site (manual counts, timed tracers, observer logs, at least five operating days including two peaks) and proposed acceptance targets, to agree contractually.

## 2026-09-28: D5 Technical Architecture (approved)

- .NET event-driven system on Kafka and PostgreSQL with TimescaleDB, deployed on-prem per authority per airport.
- Ariva ingests tracks and owns versioned zone geometry; vendor crossings are a fallback. ADR-0003.
- Nine bounded contexts; Border Integration and Flight Demand are anti-corruption layers.
- Stream processing in plain .NET consumer services with keyed state. ADR-0005.
- PostgreSQL with TimescaleDB Community Edition (licence checked for self-managed use); plain partitioning as fallback. ADR-0006.
- Four aggregate-only AMAN contracts: DeskSessionChanged, DeskIntervalStats, EGateIntervalStats, InboundFlightLaneDemand, with timestamps coarsened to the interval. ADR-0010.
- Python worker for model training and batch forecasts (v1); staffing optimiser in .NET. ADR-0013.
- Canonical flight model with AIDX and ACRIS adapters, mocks and a replay harness. ADR-0014.
- Signed, offline-verifiable licence file per deployment for module licensing.
- Hosted SaaS by Dalil is out of scope for now.
- Rebus for commands and sagas. Superseded on 2026-10-01 (Rebus not used).
- EF Core for relational schemas. Superseded on 2026-10-01 (NHibernate).
- Observability on AMAN's Prometheus, Grafana and Loki. Superseded on 2026-10-01 (Serilog and OpenTelemetry to SigNoz or Loki).
- OpenSearch optional for event and audit search. Narrowed on 2026-10-01 (not used in Phase 0 or the MVP).
- Unprefixed topic names (for example `tracks.samples.v1`). Superseded on 2026-10-01 (`ariva.<context>.<event>.v1`).

- Integration clients (ARV-042), decided 2026-10-03: `X-TOTP-Code` on data calls is checked within the TOTP window but without the replay guard (the token exchange keeps it), so a client is not limited to one call per 30 seconds; a client has at least one allowed source network, and an exchange from outside them is refused before anything is counted, so only an address inside the client's networks can wear it down; per-client attempts over the limit (5 a minute) are refused without counting toward the lockout, which caps how fast even an inside attacker reaches ten failures; the failure count starts again at the lock and an expired lock is not renewed by a failure in the same call; a lock stops new token exchanges only, so tokens already held keep working until they expire (an attacker on the client's network cannot cut a running feed by failing on purpose); every change to a client (scopes, sites, networks, policy, secret, seed, disable) moves its token version, every token carries the version it was issued for, and a token of another version is refused on its next call; the scope check reads the client's scopes and sites from the database on every call, not from the token; clients are disabled, never deleted; two connectivity check endpoints let integrators test scope and site binding before the data endpoints of ARV-043 exist.
- Integration API batches (ARV-043), decided 2026-10-03: every Integration API path names the site (`/api/v1/integration/sites/{siteCode}/...`), so the site binding is checked before the action and never read from a body; answers follow Ariva's API convention (data, RFC 9457 problems) rather than AMAN's `Result<T>` envelope, and a batch answers 200 with per-item results even when items were refused; `Idempotency-Key` is required, kept 24 hours per client, and the same key with another body, endpoint or site is 422 (the IETF Idempotency-Key draft's choice); each client is its own feed (`api-` and its id), so freshness and the stale-feed alarm are per client and a body cannot write into another feed; batch bodies are read strictly (exact camelCase names, no unknown or repeated members, depth 8). batches are limited per host (8 at once and 16 waiting, before authentication and before any body is read, so a burst of 1 MB bodies cannot exhaust memory or the temporary volume) and per client (120 a minute per replica, counted only after the token is validated, so a forged token cannot spend another client's allowance), and a body within the limit is buffered in memory; the api-integration ingress takes at most 1 MB. Open: a unit of work disposed without being ended commits what was promised (pre-existing; the API's filter ends it explicitly, so the batch endpoints roll back on an exception).
- AIDX 22.1 inbound (ARV-044), decided 2026-10-03: validated against Ariva's own AIDX profile (typed elements Ariva reads, the rest accepted unread), because IATA's XSDs are licensed and not in the repository; customers licensed for them can add them (To confirm per deployment); the direction comes from the site's airports, so one AIDX feed can carry both arrivals and departures and legs of other airports are refused per leg; the flight key is airline, number, suffix, origin date and direction, shared with the JSON route's examples so both routes update one leg; the answer is an `IATA_AIDX_FlightLegRS` acknowledgement in the OTA pattern (shape To confirm per AODB); the root `TimeStamp` is required and is the message time, so a re-delivered old message is older than what it would overwrite and changes nothing (without it, a late re-delivery stamped with its receipt time would overwrite newer values), which is why `Idempotency-Key` can be optional for AIDX (many AODBs cannot set headers); check-in counters are not read from AIDX; operation times map onto the leg's milestone fields (with per-field message-time ordering, the same UTC and near-schedule rules and the same per-key lock) rather than separate `FlightEvent` rows, a deviation from the story's wording: what is lost is a per-milestone history row and the events route's "send the leg first" check, neither of which an AIDX leg needs since it carries its own identity. Order: ARV-044, ARV-045 and ARV-048 are built before the deferred emulators of ARV-029, with the test acting as the partner; ARV-029 then points its emulators at the finished endpoints.
- Outbound endpoints (ARV-045), decided 2026-10-03: the SSRF control is in the connection itself (`ConnectCallback` resolves, checks every address and dials only a checked one), not in a check before the request, so DNS rebinding cannot pass; no proxy for outbound calls (a proxy would hide the address actually reached; a deployment that requires one needs a design change); resilience is a small per-endpoint handler rather than a new library dependency; outbound endpoints reuse the integration permissions instead of new ones; the authentication kind of an endpoint is fixed for its life; the ACRIS flight profile follows the ACRIS Semantic Model names Ariva could confirm (To confirm per AODB); ARV-045 is built before the ARV-029 emulator, with the test's stand-in AODB; the two outbound constructions the scanner would flag (SEC-020 `new HttpClient` in OutboundClients, SEC-023 `new SocketsHttpHandler` in OutboundTransport) are allowlist entries awaiting the product owner's approval, not silent exemptions; moving an endpoint needs its secret again; TLS chains are built without certificate or revocation downloads.
- SSIM import (ARV-046), decided 2026-10-03: preview, then import only what was previewed, bound by a Data Protection preview token (file SHA-256, site, horizon, window, user, two hours; nothing is stored between the two); a schedule is a fallback feed by ownership, not by message time: each leg records the feed that set its schedule and whether it is a fallback (script 0029), a fallback creates legs and updates only fallback-owned ones, and a live feed always replaces a fallback's schedule (an earlier draft back-dated SSIM messages 29 days, which let an import overwrite a leg a live feed had pre-loaded more than 29 days earlier; replaced after the security review); a failed unit is rolled back and every import is audited in its own transaction with its outcome; the SSIM key of a time mode U arrival uses the UTC departure date while AIDX keys by the origin's local date, so near midnight one flight could become two legs (To confirm before pilot); legs expand within a 1 to 200 day horizon (default 60) rather than the whole season; units of 500 legs, each in its own transaction; a new `FlightSchedule` permission (System administrator); the arrival date is derived from the times rather than SSIM's date variation field (To confirm against airline files).

- Passenger displays (ARV-058), decided 2026-10-04: a display is a site's record (script 0035) with its settings and its entries as JSON (queue zone, label per language), audited without its credential; the player authenticates with its own display credential (`ardp_`, 256 random bits, prefix and SHA-256 stored like a device's, constant-time comparison) through a scheme of its own (`Ariva.Display`), never a user session, and polls every 10 seconds rather than joining the live hub (the hub serves users); the board answer carries each entry's nowcast with its age by the server's clock, and the band, the hysteresis, the wider band for a degraded nowcast and the stale fallback are the player's; the player's address carries the credential in the fragment. Permissions `Display.*` go to terminal duty managers and border shift supervisors (a border deployment has no duty manager) and administrators; creating a display and a new credential are critical actions (step-up MFA); changing a display's settings, labels or zones and deleting it are not, since neither reveals nor issues a credential and both are audited, and a deleted display's player is refused at once. Board languages are English, Arabic, Portuguese and Swahili; the Portuguese is European Portuguese wording and the Swahili is standard Swahili, both To confirm with a native reviewer per site.
- Users and access screen (ARV-059), decided 2026-10-04: one administrator screen at `/users` (`User.Search`) with an Accounts tab and an Audit log tab (`AuditEntry.Search`); roles change as their boxes change rather than through a save, since each grant and revoke is its own audited, step-up action on the server; an administrator's own account is read only on the screen (the server still lets an administrator correct its own name and email through the API). From the security review: an account is created with its sites in the same request (`CreateUserRequest.AllSites`, `SiteCodes`), a site-limited administrator must give one of its own sites, and an account without sites, whatever its roles, is administrable and visible in the audit only to all-sites administrators, because a site administrator who could reset an account during the step between creation and sites could sign in as it once it held wider sites; enabling a disabled account is a critical action (step-up), like the resets, since it gives access back. Account names are now one line of visible text (no control, invisible or broken characters except the zero-width non-joiner and joiner, at most 200), and the audit summary of an account quotes the name and email as JSON strings, so a name such as `x; roles=SystemAdministrator` cannot read as a field of the trail (CWE-117); summaries written before this keep their old form.

(2) A part bin (counting started more than 5 seconds into it, or a pause of more than 30 seconds between the screen's clock ticks: the tablet slept or the screen was in the background) is shown and never sent; the API has no way to mark a count as partial. (3) One tracer run per batch (the API takes up to 20): one key per run keeps the retry rule simple. (4) Unsent items stay in memory only (no offline tablet without an ADR): they belong to the account that queued them and are dropped unsent at sign-out, at the end of the session or when another account signs in, and when the screen is left (after a Stay or Leave anyway prompt); a send without an answer within 30 seconds counts as lost and is sent again with the same key after 10 seconds, doubling to 5 minutes, and at once on Send again now (security review M1 and L3). (5) A correction whose outcome is unknown (no answer, 408, 429 or 5xx) freezes its inputs and is only sent again as it is with the same key, or cancelled; a 409 for a reused key says the correction was not saved and shows the counts again (security review M2). - Observer tablet desk log (ARV-104d), built 2026-10-09, with these points proposed by the implementation (the engineer), pending the owner (wiki 12, Validation capture step 5 and 6; wiki 07 section 8): (1) The desk log's minutes follow Ariva's clock, read from the HTTP Date header of Ariva's answers (as ARV-104d's note asked), while the line tally keeps the tablet's own clock (point 1 of the ARV-104c entry): two rules on one screen until the owner chooses. Recommended: align the tally to Ariva's clock as well (one rule, no API change). (2) How Ariva's clock is read: the Date header has whole seconds, so the tablet takes the middle of that second against its own clock when the answer came, ignores answers that took more than 10 seconds, uses the latest such answer (the running list on opening, every desk read and batch, every count and tracer answer), and reads it again (the running list) at once when the tablet's own clock jumps by more than 5 seconds against the time that passed (set by hand or from the network, or after sleep), when the screen comes back into view, and every 10 seconds while it waits for a reading. Within about a second, which is enough for minutes; the screen warns above a minute of difference. (3) An observer logs at most 20 desks at once, the API's batch limit, chosen from the campaign's desks; a campaign with more desks needs several observers. Alternative: several batches per bin from one tablet. (4) Stop logging sends the minutes of the bin that have ended at once (a part bin, which the API accepts, ARV-104b point 8); the minute in progress is not sent, and a restart within the same bin sends only the minutes not yet sent, as a second batch of that bin. (5) An earlier minute of the bin in progress can be filled in or changed until the bin is sent; afterwards only through a correction with a reason. There is no one-tap "no change for every desk" button: each desk needs its own tap each minute. Alternative, if the KPI annex accepts it: a "same as last minute" button for the desks not tapped, which records states the observer did not tap one by one. (6) A correction must choose a state other than the one recorded. (7) For corrections the tablet reads the observer's latest 500 desk minutes (20 desks for 25 minutes, or 4 desks for two hours); older minutes are not reachable from the tablet.
