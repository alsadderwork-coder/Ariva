# Ariva formulas

Every computation Ariva performs, with symbols, units, inputs, outputs, edge cases and test cases. Each formula should live as a pure function in Ariva.Core (or the Python worker for v1 forecasting) with unit tests that include the test cases below.

Sources: D4 (How It Works) and D5 (Technical Architecture), 2026-09-28. Values marked "reference" come from the prototype's simulator (the reference scenario, seed 9303) and are defaults for Ariva.Simulation.Api, not contractual or field-validated numbers. Items marked Proposed are design proposals made here where the sources state the rule but not its exact form; items marked To confirm need a decision.

## Conventions

| Quantity | Unit | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Time instants | UTC, sensor or source clock (event time) | `DateTimeOffset` |
| Waits | minutes in formulas; `TimeSpan` in code | `TimeSpan` |
| Service and cycle times | seconds in AMAN contracts; minutes in formulas | `TimeSpan` |
| Rates (throughput, arrivals) | passengers per minute | `double` |
| Counts per interval | passengers | `double` in forecasts (fluid model), `int` in measurement |
| Geometry | metres, degrees | `double` |
| Bin size delta | 15 minutes unless stated | `TimeSpan` |

Rounding: never call a ceiling on a floating-point ratio directly. Ratios that are exact integers in decimal can land one ulp above or below (for example (24 - 0.3) / (4.25 - 0.3) evaluates to 5.999999999999999). Use `Math.Ceiling(Math.Round(x, 9))` or `decimal` arithmetic. Compare floating results in tests with a tolerance of 1e-9.

## F1. Sensor footprint from mounting height and field of view

Source: D4.

```
W = 2 * (h - z) * tan(alpha / 2)
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| h | Mounting height of the sensor | m |
| z | Height of the tracking plane: head height for counting, 0 (floor) for coverage drawings | m |
| alpha | Field of view along one axis | degrees |
| W | Footprint width along that axis at plane z | m |

Apply once per axis to get the footprint L x W. Vendor footprint tables are authoritative because they include tracking margins; this formula is for sanity checks and committee discussion.

Edge cases: h <= z is invalid (sensor below the plane); alpha must be in (0, 180). Tilt is not modelled (Xovis allows up to 15 degrees on one axis and 5 on the other).

Tests: h = 4, z = 0, alpha = 90 gives W = 8.0. h = 4, z = 1.7, alpha = 90 gives W = 4.6.

## F2. Sensor count for a rectangular zone with overlap

Source: D4.

```
N = ceil((L - o) / (a - o)) * ceil((B - o) / (b - o))
N_min = min(N(a, b), N(b, a))
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| L, B | Zone length and width | m |
| a, b | Sensor footprint length and width (vendor table) | m |
| o | Required overlap between neighbours; at least 0.3 for Xovis multisensor setups | m |
| N_min | Sensor count; try both footprint orientations and keep the smaller | count |

Edge cases: a <= o or b <= o is invalid. If L <= a the first factor is 1. Apply the rounding rule above. For LiDAR the binding constraint is occlusion, not range: plan so every point of a dense queue is seen by at least two sensors from different angles; the perception vendor's simulation sets the count (planning heuristic 2 to 4 per zone).

Tests (D4 worked example: 24 x 12 m snake plus 4 m overflow band = 24 x 16 m zone, o = 0.3):

| Ceiling | Footprint a x b | Expected N_min |
|---|---|---|
| 3 m | 9.6 x 9.6 (wide footprint) | 6 |
| 3 m | 3.75 x 2.13 (standard) | 63 (other orientation 65) |
| 4 m | 10 x 10 | 6 |
| 4 m | 7 x 4.25 | 16 (other orientation 18) |
| 12 m | 12 x 9 (high mount) | 6 |
| 18 m | 10.5 x 8 (ultra high) | 8 (other orientation 9) |

## F3. BOQ planning footprints and the area helper

Source: the BOQ in the business plan and its prototype calculator (D7, D8); given in the build brief. Used for bills of quantities before a site survey, not for installation design.

Planning footprints by ceiling height:

| Ceiling | Planning footprint L x W |
|---|---|
| 4 to 6 m | 10 x 10 m |
| 10 to 14 m | 12 x 9 m |
| Other heights | Use the vendor table (F2 tests give 3 m and 18 m examples) |

Area helper:

```
N = ceil( A / ((L - 0.3) * (W - 0.3)) * 1.3 )
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| A | Zone area | m2 |
| L, W | Planning footprint | m |
| 0.3 | Overlap deducted from each footprint dimension | m |
| 1.3 | Planning margin for layout shape, obstructions and edges (named "overlap" in the prototype data) | factor |

The prototype computes `Math.ceil(area / cell * 1.3 - 1e-9)`; the 1.3 is inside the ceiling.

Edge cases: A = 0 gives 0; the helper ignores zone shape, so it can disagree with F2 by one or two sensors; the BOQ may override it by judgement.

## F4. Line crossing (Proposed implementation)

Source: D5 requires Ariva to compute crossings centrally from tracks on versioned lines; the method below is Proposed.

For consecutive samples p1 at t1 and p2 at t2 of one track, and a line segment from q1 to q2, a crossing occurs when p1 and p2 are on opposite sides of the line and the segment p1 p2 intersects the segment q1 q2. The side of a point is the sign of the 2D cross product of (q2 - q1) and (p - q1); a point exactly on the line keeps the side of the previous sample. Each line stores which side is inside: moving from the outside to the inside is `In`, the reverse is `Out`. The crossing time is interpolated:

```
s = |p1 - x| / |p1 - p2|          (x = intersection point)
t_cross = t1 + s * (t2 - t1)
```

Rules:

- A track touching the line without passing it is not a crossing.
- Jitter: when a track crosses a line and back within a debounce window, keep only the net crossing (debounce window To confirm, Proposed 2 s).
- Lines must be at least 1 m inside sensor coverage (D4 site-survey rule).

Tests: line from (-1, 0) to (1, 0). Samples (0, -1) at 0 s and (0, 1) at 2 s give one crossing at 1.0 s. Samples (0, -1), (0, 0), (0, -1) give no crossing (touch and return). Samples (2, -1) and (2, 1) give no crossing (outside the segment).

The future track-crossing code (D5: crossings computed centrally from tracks) applies the side-of-line rule above itself: the side is the sign of the cross product, a sample exactly on the line keeps the side of the previous sample, and a track that touches the line without passing it is not a crossing. It does not use `Geometry.SegmentsIntersect` (below), which answers another question: whether two closed segments meet, a touch included. A touch must count for zone validity and a footprint's reach, and must not count as a crossing. Today counts come from vendor crossing events, so no count depends on either.

### Segment intersection for zone validity and footprint reach (ARV-161)

`Geometry.SegmentsIntersect(p1, p2, q1, q2)` serves `IsSimplePolygon` (zone validity, F22) and `Overlaps` (a device footprint reaching its zone, ARV-021). It is true when the closed segments touch or cross:

```
touch     an end of either segment is closer than Tolerance (1 cm) to the other segment (DistanceToSegment < 0.01 m):
          a shared end, an end on the other segment, a collinear overlap, a segment of no length on the other
cross     otherwise, each segment's ends lie on opposite sides of the other's line, each at least 1 cm from it:
          side(a, b, r) = sign of cross(b - a, r - a), and 0 when |cross| < 0.01 x |b - a|
```

A cross product is a length squared, so it is compared with the tolerance times the segment's length: the point's distance to the line against 1 cm. Before any arithmetic, the ends of each segment are put in order (by x, then y) and then the two segments, so all eight orders and directions of the arguments compute with the same numbers and give one answer, rounding included. An end within 1 cm of the other's line but beyond its end has side 0 and gives no crossing; segments that do cross then always have a touch, because the part between that end and the crossing passes within 1 cm of the other segment's end. So `IsSimplePolygon` refuses a vertex on, or closer than 1 cm to, an edge that is not its own, and `Overlaps` counts rings that only touch, or come within 1 cm, as overlapping, in any vertex order. Before ARV-161 a side was 0 only for a cross product below 1e-12 m2 (in effect exactly zero), so at an exact touch the order of the points decided the answer (CsCheck seed `cCcbgdLcNjA6`), and a vertex 1 to 9 mm beside another edge passed as simple.

Tests: `GeometryTests`, each case in all eight orders or every rotation and direction of the rings: the seed's segments sharing an end at the origin (3.5e-14 m apart), true; an end 9 mm from the other segment true, 11 mm false; collinear segments 9 mm apart true, 11 mm false; an end on the other segment in decimals binary cannot hold, true, also a kilometre from the origin; an end 9 mm from the other's line beyond its end with the segments crossing, true; segments of no length; a ring with a vertex on another edge, 5 mm from it or a spike folding back 1 mm beside an earlier edge, not simple, 2 cm from it, simple; a footprint 5 mm from its zone overlapping, 2 cm from it not. Properties below.

## F5. Realised wait

Source: D4.

Per track (primary method):

```
w_i = t_exit_i - t_entry_i
```

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| t_entry_i | Time of the first inward crossing of any entry line of the queue, including the overflow entry line (overflow time counts as wait) |
| t_exit_i | Time of the outward crossing of the exit line after t_entry_i |
| w_i | Realised wait of track i, minutes |

Excluded from realised wait: tracks with no exit (abandoned, fragmented, censored; see F6), tracks originating behind the desk line or from staff doors (staff exclusion rule).

FIFO method from crossings (fallback when tracks fragment, when only vendor line crossings are available, and in the fluid simulation): in a first-in, first-out snake the n-th entrant is the n-th to exit.

```
e_1 <= e_2 <= ...   entry crossing times, sorted
x_1 <= x_2 <= ...   exit crossing times, sorted
w_n = x_n - e_n
```

Equivalently, with cumulative entries A(t) and exits D(t), the wait of the n-th entrant is D^-1(n) - A^-1(n) (the simulator uses this form on fluid per-minute curves, interpolating within the minute).

Edge cases:

- w_i < 0 means a clock or geometry error: reject the track and raise a data-quality event.
- FIFO alignment breaks with abandonment, lane switching and count errors. Re-anchor both sequences whenever the queue is observed empty (occupancy 0); flag waits computed after a conservation residual above tolerance as `Degraded`.
- Fragmentation shortens waits (biases low); it is measured by the track completion rate (F18).

Tests: track entering at 18:00:00 and exiting at 18:12:30 gives 12.5 min. FIFO: entries at 0, 1, 2 min and exits at 5, 6, 8 min give waits 5, 5, 6. Interval readings (ARV-173): one T1 push of 17:03 with 5 in on the entry line and 3 out on the exit line gives 17:03 entries 5 and exits 3, on the lines and in the zone, with the lines in either order in the push or in two pushes, and nothing in 17:04; an empty queue with 2 in and 3 out in a minute gives one unmatched exit and two waits, as time order does (`IntervalInstantTests`).

Implementation (ARV-030, `Ariva.Core/Queueing/QueueStateEngine`): one engine per queue zone, pure (no I/O, time passed in) and bounded in state, work and output whatever a device sends: at most 20,000 people held, 100,000 events buffered (a tenth of it for events ahead of the clock), 20,000 resolved tracks remembered, 64 devices tracked apart and 100,000 records per step by default (a full step ends early and the next continues); an interval reading counts at most 5,000 people, and each event costs the number of devices or a logarithm, never the number of people. Events are held in a reorder buffer and processed in event-time order when the watermark passes them. The watermark runs on event time (ARV-148, the owner's decision of 2026-10-10 on finding F1 of the ARV-104j rehearsal): it is the zone's clock less the allowed lateness, where the zone's clock is the latest event time the zone has accepted, each event counting at most up to Ariva's receive time of the batch that carried it (its own batch's, even when the host has ticked the idle zone on to a later time since, so the live path and a replay of the same records agree), and never more than the push interval behind the zone's latest receive time (so a zone whose devices send no events still closes its minutes; before its first event a zone has nothing to close and does not move). The lateness and the push interval are set per site (`Stream:LatenessSeconds` and `Stream:PushIntervalSeconds`, overridden in `Stream:Sites:<code>`); the lateness is at least the push interval, since a push carries events up to that old, and is 90 s by default (the one-minute push interval and the 30 s allowed before for transport and device clocks; Proposed), the push interval 60 s. The watermark never passes the receive time less the lateness, whatever the buffer holds. An event at or behind the watermark is late, counted with the earliest late time, and not applied (a recomputation decides, F6), as before. Events of one instant are applied in a fixed order of their content (interval readings, those of an entry line first by the zone's geometry (ARV-173), then crossings, readings and track samples; then line or zone, direction, track, interval start, counts and flag) and only then in arrival order, so the batches, topics and partitions that carried them never change a result: the same events split into other batches or reordered across topics give the same final bins, minutes and line counts whenever each push's events lie within the lateness of the zone's clock (QueueEngineWatermarkTests). Interval readings come first, so the order of an instant's application is fixed; their spread no longer depends on it (below). Events more than 5 minutes ahead of Ariva's clock are refused; events less far ahead are held until the watermark reaches them but move the zone's clock only up to their receive time (CWE-501: a device whose clock runs ahead cannot close the other devices' minutes early). With the receive clock (push interval 0, the rule before ARV-148) a one-minute push of events up to 61 s old turned its older crossings late whenever another batch of the push, or another device's push, was applied first: about 40 percent of a busy lane's crossings in the ARV-104j rehearsal. An interval reading ending at T is spread over the part of it after the last event processed before T's instant and after the watermark (the k-th of n people at start + (T minus start)(k + 0.5)/n, never later than one tick before T), so it never reaches into minutes already processed, and no event of its own instant narrows it: readings that end together (the entry and exit lines of one T1 push, two pushes of one minute, or two devices) each spread over their own interval, whatever their order in a push, across pushes or topics (ARV-173; before it, the second reading of an instant was spread over nothing and all its people landed at T, the next minute: one push of 17:03 had its exits counted in 17:04). An entrant is counted in the minute its reading places it; the held entry time that FIFO pairing uses never goes before a person already held (a second entry-line reading of the same instant, of more than a minute, is held from the first one's last entry on). Within an instant the readings of entry lines are applied before the others by the zone's geometry, not by line names, and a counted exit whose FIFO partner entered after it is unmatched (counted as an exit, no wait; that person stays held): in time order nobody counted in was waiting then, so the pairs are those time order gives whatever the lines are called, and a counted exit never makes a negative wait. A step that fills inside an instant (ARV-114d) leaves the rest of the instant's readings spread over the tick before T, still in their own last minute. With one-minute pushes the watermark is about 30 s before a minute's end when its readings are applied (90 s lateness), so T1 counts lie in the last 30 s of their own minute. Movements are reported as entries and exits per minute. A tracked exit closes its own track (method Track); any other exit pairs with the earliest person held (method Fifo; with interval counts spread evenly over their interval this is the cumulative-curve method, Cumulative, for T1 devices), except that a tracked exit never takes a person the same device is tracking (track keys are namespaced by device), so tracks that entered before the engine started do not displace others. Anonymous crossings of an overflow band's entry line are not counted, because the same person crosses the queue's entry line later; overflow time is counted only for tracked people. Re-anchoring happens when every zone of the queue (the queue zone and its overflow bands) reads zero, checked once every event of the reading's instant has been applied: the zone and its bands are often read by different devices at the same instant and their batches arrive in any order, so a band's zero beside the zone's previous reading is never taken for an empty queue (ARV-139b). When a full step ends inside such an instant, the check is not run with part of the instant applied: it stays pending in the engine's state and in the zone snapshot (`QueueEngineState.PendingAnchorUtc`, zone snapshot version 8) and runs in the next step once the rest of the instant has been applied, so a split instant gives what an unsplit one gives (ARV-114d). A pending check is always the instant one tick after the watermark (where the full step stopped, so that no unprocessed event of it can become late), the time of the last processed event and the time of a reading of the queue the engine holds; a snapshot that says otherwise, or that is older than version 8 and carries one, is refused (CWE-501), and an older snapshot restores with no check pending. A residual of more than 2 people flags the FIFO waits that follow as `Degraded` until the next clean anchor. Inputs flagged by the sensing pipeline (a skewed or corrected clock) make the waits they touch `Degraded` (F11). On the reference evening, cumulative-curve waits from Xovis minute counts are within a minute of the tracked waits (QueueEngineScenarioTests).

Repeated batches (ARV-168; the window and the bounds Proposed): Ingest gives every sensing batch an id (`SensingBatchIds`, ARV-174) derived from the device, the kind, the vendor's package ids and the kind's events as the device sent them (for anonymous crossings in a push without a package also the device's send time, or the receipt time; for a kind stamped with Ariva's receipt time: the body with the package ids, else with the receipt time; docs/architecture/sensor-adapters.md), so a device that sends a push again whose request it dropped (with its send time stamped again too, except for anonymous crossings in a push without a package), or a record Kafka delivers twice, repeats the id; the id is on the batch, not on its events. A queue zone (`ZoneProcessor` with `RecentBatches`) applies a batch of one device with one id once:

```
key(b)    = (device code of b, batch id of b)          never the id alone
latest(b) = max event time of b, over its events at most MaxAhead (5 min) after b's receive time
until(b)  = latest(b) + lateness                      remembered while watermark < until(b)
```

A batch whose key is remembered is dropped whole: it still says its device was heard and moves the zone's receive clock, exactly as a health report at its receive time would, but none of its events reach the queue, line, overflow or desk engines again (counted as `RepeatedBatches`). With the defaults a push's key lives about three minutes after its receipt: until the zone's clock is two latenesses (180 s) past the push's latest event, since the watermark trails the clock by one. The second lateness covers a resend whose times Ingest corrected differently, by up to the lateness (another replica's clock estimate); once the watermark is past `until(b)` every event of a repeat is behind it, so the repeat is late, counted as late and never applied, and the archive, which drops it for three days, never gives it to a recomputation either. An id the device uses again after the window counts again (for example a buggy producer's wrapped counter); within the window a reused id is taken for the same batch. A batch with an empty id and health reports are never remembered. Bounded (CWE-120): at most `MaxRememberedBatches` keys per zone (20,000, from 1 to 200,000) and `MaxRememberedBatchesPerDevice` per device (1,000, at most the zone's bound; a device pushing all four kinds every second holds about 720); beyond a bound the key whose window ends first (then by device and id) is forgotten and counted (`ForgottenBatches`), so a device that floods its zone forgets only its own keys, and a resend of a forgotten batch would be applied again. The keys are in the zone snapshot (`ZoneProcessorState.RecentBatches`, version 10, in the order they would be forgotten), so a zone restored between a batch and its resend still drops the resend; a snapshot of an earlier version carries none (one that says otherwise is refused) and restores remembering none, so a batch applied before the upgrade and resent within one window after it counts again. A snapshot's keys are checked before use (CWE-501): device codes as the registry writes them, non-empty ids, each key once, a window ending after the watermark and no later than the receive clock plus `MaxAhead` and the lateness, and the bounds. Tracked crossings keep their own rule beside it: a track's first entry counts and a later entry or exit of an open or resolved track (`<device code>/<track id>`, 20,000 resolved tracks remembered per zone, oldest first) is a duplicate, because it holds beyond resends too (one person crossing an entry line twice); both rules are scoped by device within the zone, and within a batch's window the batch rule drops a resent crossing batch before the track rule sees it. Tests: `RepeatedBatchTests` (the same batch twice counts once and is a health report otherwise; two devices with one id both count; within and after the window; an id reused after it; the bounds; snapshot and restore) and `QueueStreamResendTests` (a push resent through Ingest with the same batch id applies its interval once in the stream's minute counts). Preconditions (security review of ARV-168, closed by ARV-174 except where stated): a resend is recognised when it carries the same events under the same package ids, or for a kind stamped with the receipt time the same body and package ids; a send time the device stamps again on a retry (`sentUtc`, `sensor_info.time`) and the layout of the body do not matter, except for anonymous crossings without a package (residual 6). What remains: (1) a declarative section stamped at receipt whose mapping names no package gets a new id on every receipt, so a resent message is counted again (for anonymous crossings a double count; the conformance kit warns about such crossings); (2) a section stamped at receipt with a package relies on the vendor's counter: if the counter restarts and repeats a byte-identical message within three days (the archive's window; the queue engine's is minutes), the message is taken for a resend and dropped; a send time in the body (`sentTime`) prevents that, but is then part of the body, so a device that stamps it again on a retry is not recognised; (3) the queue engine remembers a batch until its latest event is a lateness behind the watermark, so a message stamped at receipt and resent later than that (about three minutes with the defaults) is applied again live, its events stamped at the new receipt, while the archive keeps it once (a replay or recomputation counts it once); (4) a resend that straddles the upgrade to ARV-174 is counted again once, its first send having had an id of the earlier form; (5) in a declarative mapping with positions in the device's frame, a change of the device's registered position or orientation between a send and its resend changes the mapped positions and so the tracks' id (tracks repeat harmlessly: a track's first entry counts); (6) anonymous crossings (no track id) have no identity of their own: two people crossing one line in the same second look alike, and a counter that sends each crossing as its own message would have them merged. So in a push without a package whose crossings include one without a track id, the crossings' id also hashes the device's send time, or the receipt time when the push has none (ARV-174 security review, R1): a retry that keeps its send time is recognised, one that stamps it again is counted again (as before ARV-174), and without a send time every resend is counted again; two messages with the same anonymous crossings and the same send time (to the precision of the device's clock) are still merged, as they were before ARV-174, being the same message byte for byte, and so are the messages of a device whose clock is frozen or jumps back and repeats the same observations (its clock state, Corrected or Unreliable, is the operator's signal). Send a `packageId` (or track ids) with anonymous crossings and a retry is recognised whatever its send time; (7) interval counts are taken as one report per line and interval per device: a gateway that forwards two sensors counting one line under one credential, both reporting the same counts for the same interval without a package, would have the second merged; register each sensor as a device of its own (or send a package id).

## F6. Attribution to the entry bin; provisional and final

Source: D4, D5; timeout and censoring rules Proposed.

Attribution: a wait belongs to the bin containing its entry time.

```
bin(i) = floor((t_entry_i - t_day_start) / delta)    bins are [b, b + delta)
```

Track outcomes for a track that crossed an entry line:

| Outcome | Condition | In realised wait? |
|---|---|---|
| `Exited` | Crossed the exit line | Yes |
| `Abandoned` | Left the queue zone without crossing the exit line (back over an entry line or across a non-exit boundary) | No; counts in the abandonment rate |
| `Fragmented` | Track lost inside the zone and not recovered within the hand-over window | No; lowers the track completion rate |
| `Censored` | Still unresolved T_censor after entry | No; counted as censored, bin flagged |

A bin b is final when all of these hold, and provisional otherwise:

1. The bin has ended and the watermark has passed b + delta (the watermark is the zone's latest event time less the lateness allowance, tuned per site: D5, F5), and every device outage minute in the bin is known (the stream keeps a bin provisional until the receive clock less the push interval has passed its end, ARV-148).
2. Every track that entered in b has an outcome (`Exited`, `Abandoned`, `Fragmented` or `Censored`). This is D4's "everyone who entered has exited", made explicit for tracks that never exit.
3. The zone's data quality for the bin is known (a sensor outage covering an entry or exit line makes the bin `Unknown`; it still becomes final, with that flag).

Timeout (Proposed, To confirm): T_censor = 120 min by default, configurable per zone profile. A bin finalised with censored tracks is flagged `Degraded` if censored tracks exceed 5% of its entries (threshold Proposed).

Revisions: a late event, a correction or a recomputation produces revision r + 1 of the bin; results are upserted by (zone, bin start, revision) and earlier revisions are kept. Each revision records `ZoneProfileVersion`.

Use: reports show provisional bins marked as such; SLA evaluation uses final bins only.

Edge cases: a bin with no entries is final once condition 1 holds, with count 0 and no wait statistics. A bin that a recomputation changes after it was used in a finalised evaluation produces a new evaluation revision, never an in-place change.

Tests: a passenger entering at 18:14:30 and exiting at 18:40:00 belongs to bin 18:00; that bin cannot be final before 18:40:00 plus the lateness allowance. A track entering at 18:05 and lost at 18:10 inside the zone is `Fragmented`; the bin can still become final.

Implementation (ARV-030): the engine resolves each entrant as exited (a wait), `Abandoned` (back over an entry line: the tracked person, or the latest anonymous entrant), `Fragmented` (a track with position samples not seen within the hand-over window, default 30 s, To confirm; or a tracked person still held when the queue is observed empty), `Censored` (T_censor after entry, default 120 min; or the earliest held when the zone's bound is reached) or `Reanchored` (an anonymous FIFO residual when the queue is observed empty). Bins, attribution and finality are ARV-031: `Ariva.Core/Queueing/BinAccumulator` takes the engine's steps in order and attributes each wait and resolution to the bin and minute of its entry, entries and exits to the minute they happened. Bins align to the site's day start (`DayStartOffset`; delta divides a day). A bin is final when the engine's watermark (which already allows for lateness) has passed its end, nobody who entered in it is unresolved and, in the stream, every device outage minute in it is known (ARV-148: the receive clock less the push interval has passed its end, so an outage that ends later never finds the bin final); every bin the watermark passes is published, an empty one final with count 0 and no statistics. Quality is the worst of degraded inputs, a re-anchor residual or a rejected pair, censored above 5 percent of entries, approximate percentiles and the outages the caller marks (F11). Final results never change: late events go to the bin of their minute (the engine reports them per minute, within a 3-day horizon matching the archive; older ones are only counted), a provisional bin carries them and asks for a recomputation once final, a final bin asks at once, and so does an outage marked late for a final bin (`RecomputationRequest`, one per run of contiguous bins); `BinRevisions.Next` turns a recomputation over the archive into revision r + 1 for the final bins whose values changed, with its reason. Provisional percentiles come from the histogram (cheap to refresh, exact to 30 seconds); final ones from the exact waits. Bounds: 2,000 open bins (beyond, the earliest is published final with its open people, Degraded; a watermark jump beyond them is reported as a gap), 50,000 exact waits per bin including its minutes (beyond, final percentiles come from the histogram, Degraded), 1,000 quality marks; counts are 64-bit. The day start offset is a whole number of minutes. On the reference day, Handler B's island C measured this way breaches in the 19:00, 19:15 and 19:30 bins, as the scenario does (BinScenarioTests).

## F7. Percentiles per minute and per 15-minute bin (arrival-weighted)

Source: D4 (mean, median, P90 or P95, share within target per bin); D5; prototype for the weighting rule.

Arrival-weighted means every passenger counts once, whichever minute they entered in. With individual waits, take the percentile over all waits attributed to the bin. With weighted samples (per-minute cohorts, fluid simulation, forecasts), use the weighted nearest-rank rule:

```
sort samples by wait ascending: (w_1, omega_1), (w_2, omega_2), ...
Omega = sum of omega_k
P_p = smallest w_k such that sum_{j <= k} omega_j >= p * Omega
```

For n unweighted samples this is the nearest-rank percentile: the ceil(p * n)-th smallest value.

Other bin statistics:

```
mean   = sum(omega_k * w_k) / Omega
share(T) = sum(omega_k where w_k <= T) / Omega       share within target T
```

Rules:

- A bin with fewer passengers than the contract's minimum (default 1; prototype treats less than 0.5 fluid passengers as empty) has no percentile (null), not zero.
- Never average per-minute or per-bin percentiles to get a longer window. For hourly and daily views, merge the underlying samples. Proposed: store a fixed-width wait histogram per bin (30-second buckets up to T_censor) in queue_intervals so continuous aggregates can merge histograms and percentiles stay exact to the bucket width.

Implementation (ARV-031, `WaitStatistics` and `BinAccumulator`): percentiles are the weighted nearest rank over the exact waits of a bin or minute (unweighted: the ceil(p x n)-th), with mean, P50, P90, P95 and the share within the target (15 minutes by default); fewer waits than the minimum (default 1) give null. Each bin and minute also carries a sparse histogram of 30-second buckets (up to 24 hours, the last open-ended); `WaitStatistics.Merge` adds histograms and `Percentile` reads one at the upper edge of the bucket that reaches the rank, so longer windows are exact to 30 seconds and never averaged.

Tests:

- Weighted: (w = 5 min, omega = 10), (w = 20 min, omega = 2). Omega = 12, 0.9 x 12 = 10.8; cumulative weight is 10 at w = 5 and 12 at w = 20, so P90 = 20. The average of per-minute P90s would wrongly give 12.5.
- share(15) for the weighted case = 10 / 12 = 0.833.

## F8. Nowcast

Source: D4, D5. Blending weights Proposed.

```
W_now = (Q(t) + 1) / mu(t)

mu_desk(t) = n_open(t) / c(t)
mu_exit(t) = exits in the last m minutes / m
mu(t)      = beta * mu_desk(t) + (1 - beta) * mu_exit(t)
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Q(t) | Queue length now (occupancy of the queue zone, including the overflow band) | passengers |
| n_open(t) | Desks in `Idle` or `Serving` (staffed and not paused) for this lane | desks |
| c(t) | Recent mean time per person at a desk (Q counts people, so c is per person): at AMAN sites the lane cycle time per person in working time from `DeskIntervalStats` (F10, ARV-117d), sensor-derived desk occupancy elsewhere | min |
| m | Exit-rate window ("last few minutes" in D4; Proposed 5 min) | min |
| beta | Blend weight (Proposed 0.5; tune in the pilot) | 0 to 1 |
| W_now | Nowcast wait for someone joining now | min |

D4 writes the throughput as n_open / s with s the mean service time, and also states that cycle time (service time plus walk-up gap) sets throughput and that using pure service time overstates throughput. Ariva therefore uses cycle time c. Exception at AMAN sites since ARV-117d (Proposed; the method accepted by the owner on 2026-10-07, docs/product/decisions.md): AMAN's start-to-start cycle also carries the time a desk waits for a passenger after a lull, which cannot be told from the walk-up gap in V1's one-minute statistics, so c there is AMAN's mean service time per person (F10, Lane cycle time per person), which leaves both out; where the walk-up gap is long the desk term is optimistic by its share, to be measured in the pilot. The desk term reacts the moment a desk opens or closes; the exit term damps noise. This is Little's law applied to the person at the back of the queue.

Variants:

- Fast-track merge: when priority and standard lanes share desks, the standard lane uses mu_std = r_merge * mu, where r_merge is the standard share of the shared desks.
- E-gates: mu_gate = (n_gates_in_service / c_gate) * (1 - r_reject) (see F12).
- Sensor outage on the zone: fall back to entry counts plus desk-event throughput; the result is `Degraded` and shown as a band (below).

Edge cases:

- No service: n_open = 0, or mu below 0.01 passengers per minute (reference). The nowcast is undefined (null), never infinite or zero. Screens show a neutral message; the "queue long with too few staffed desks" alert rule applies.
- Empty or near-empty queue: Q = 0 or W_now < 5 min shows the band "under 5 min".
- Unknown desks do not count in n_open, and the bin is flagged.

Display rules (D4: passenger screens show the nowcast rounded to 5-minute bands and change only when it moves a full band; never a realised number):

```
band(W) = [5 * floor(W / 5), 5 * floor(W / 5) + 5)
```

Hysteresis (Proposed exact rule): keep a reference value W_ref, set when the displayed band last changed. Change the displayed band to band(W_now) only when |W_now - W_ref| >= 5, then set W_ref = W_now.

Degraded band (reference): when the zone is degraded show [5 * floor(0.75 * W_now / 5), 5 * ceil(1.25 * W_now / 5)], widened so that it spans at least 10 min.

Stale data: when the latest nowcast is older than the staleness threshold (To confirm per site), screens switch to a neutral message instead of freezing on an old number.

Display precedence (first match wins): stale data or no service shows the neutral message; W_now at or above 120 min shows "over 120 min" (Proposed, ARV-032); a degraded zone shows the degraded band; W_now below 5 min shows "under 5 min"; otherwise the 5-minute band with hysteresis (extended in ARV-032, Proposed, to the "under 5 min" message, the degraded band and "over 120 min").

Tests:

- Same, with 18 exits in the last 5 min (mu_exit = 3.6) and beta = 0.5: mu = 3.8, W_now = 7.89 min.

A lull (1,290 s cycle after 20 idle minutes beside a 90 s one, both 90 s service): c = 1.5, W_now = 7.5, 7.89 with 18 exits (before: c = 11.5, W_now = 57.5). No documents in the window: no c, the exit term alone, flagged. An interval outside contract V1's bounds beside a good one: c from the good one, the term flagged.
- Fast track: mu = 4.0, r_merge = 0.5, Q = 29: W_now = 15 min.
- n_open = 0: null (no service).

- Degraded: W_now = 12 gives "5 to 15"; W_now = 4 gives "0 to 10".

Implementation (ARV-032, `Ariva.Core/Queueing/Nowcast`): `Nowcast.Compute` blends the desk and exit terms when both are known, uses either alone otherwise (the exit term alone is flagged `Degraded`, since it lags desk changes), applies the fast-track share and the e-gate reject rate (`Nowcast.EgateRate`, F12), and returns no number with a reason (`NothingOpen`, `ThroughputTooLow` below 0.01 a minute, `NoThroughputData`, `NoQueueLength`, `Implausible` when no finite wait results). Cycle times under 0.05 minutes (3 seconds, Proposed) count as unknown, so a bad desk feed cannot produce an infinite throughput; an unknown fast-track share or reject rate is not assumed to be 1 or 0 silently but flags the result `Degraded`. `NowcastDisplays.Next` applies the display precedence and the hysteresis above, keeping W_ref between updates. Two display rules beyond the reference (Proposed): a wait at or above 120 minutes (the reference cap) shows "over 120 min", degraded or not, since a band there would exclude the estimate, and the degraded band is clamped to 120 while keeping its 10-minute width; a throughput above 1,000 passengers a minute (Proposed) is not real and gives `Implausible`, so a corrupt exit count cannot show "under 5 min"; the hysteresis also holds the "under 5 min" message and the degraded band, so a wait hovering at 5 minutes does not flap, while a change between degraded and live always shows at once. `ExitRate` sums the engine's per-minute exits over the last m whole minutes (one hour kept). Measured on the reference evening through the emulator and the queue engine with the exit term, the Visitors nowcast is within 2 minutes of the scenario's from 18:04 to 18:10 and passes 15 minutes at 18:05 to 18:06. The desk term (ARV-064, `DeskTerms`, `DeskTermSource`): Ariva.Api.Stream reads, every 15 seconds (`Stream:DeskTermSeconds`), the closed desk minutes of the desks serving each queue it holds, matched as the immigration screen matches them (the immigration and emigration desks on the queue zone's level whose lane categories include the zone's lane; there is no explicit queue to desk link, and e-gates are not desks). n_open is the desks Idle or Serving for at least half of the latest minute any of them closed; c is AMAN's lane cycle time per person in working time over the exit window (`LaneCycle.PerPerson`, F10, since ARV-117d; it was the transaction-weighted cycle per transaction before), or none when AMAN's intervals for the lane carry no documents or all fail contract V1's bounds (the nowcast then falls back as above), and the desks' open time per transaction only when no interval statistics were read for the lane at all; an Unknown or degraded desk, or one whose minutes stopped before the others', flags the result `Degraded`, and with none open and any Unknown there is no desk term (n_open is not known, which is not "nothing open"). A term is used while its minute is at most 5 minutes older than the queue's (`DeskTermFreshMinutes`; desk minutes close about a minute after the queue's). Live data only: the term is not part of a zone's snapshot and replays never use it, so the golden replay and the bins are unchanged, while a minute replayed after a restart may get another nowcast than the first run did. Queues without AMAN desks (check-in, security, e-gates) keep the exit term alone, flagged; the screens show such a nowcast as an estimate ("≈") rather than as degraded data, which they keep for a stale zone or a degraded queue length.

Shadow nowcast (ARV-117, `ShadowNowcasts`, `DeskTerms.SensorOnly`): because the desk term is live only and never replayed, the pilot's comparison of the nowcast error with and without AMAN inputs cannot be rebuilt later, so the stream computes, for every live queue minute, a second W_now with `Nowcast.Compute` and stores it in `queue_minute_shadow` (ARV-117a, script 0043; keyed like `queue_minute` and written in the same checkpoint transaction as the published row; ARV-117's script 0041 held it as three columns of `queue_minute`, copied and dropped by 0043), which the runtime role can write but not read (only the role `ariva_validation_reader` reads it). Both start from the same queue-side input (Q, the exits in the last m minutes, m, the queue's flags); the published nowcast takes the desk term as above, the shadow its sensor-only part, so the two inputs differ only in n_open, c and the desk term's flag (the queue nowcast takes no AMAN reject rate and no fast-track share). Sensor-only n_open counts the desks open for at least half of the term's minute by their staff and service zones alone. Since ARV-117a a desk with staff or service zones has its minute from the site's sensor-only desk engine (`desk_sensor_minute`, F10 below), which never sees AMAN: it counts open when the zones made it Idle or Serving for at least 30 seconds, and flags the term when that minute is flagged or Unknown for at least 30 seconds, whatever AMAN said; so at a site where every desk has an AMAN code, n_open comes from the zones while AMAN is live, and the published term is unchanged (`ShadowNowcastTests`: with six AMAN desks, c = 1.5 and Q = 29 the published nowcast stays 7.5 minutes, 7.89 with 18 exits, while the shadow's term is n_open = 6 from the zones; with the zones showing every desk closed the shadow is NothingOpen). A desk without that minute (no zones, or a minute written before ARV-117a) keeps ARV-117's reading of `desk_minute.sensor_derived_seconds` (F10 rows 6 and 7, ARV-116): a desk open only through AMAN (a session or a recent transaction) is not seen by the sensors and is treated as an Unknown desk is: it flags the term, and with no desk open from the sensors there is no sensor-only term (accepted by the owner on 2026-10-07). Cycle time: until ARV-117b none (the owner's decision of 2026-10-07 for ARV-117: AMAN's interval statistics and the transactions the desk minutes count both come from AMAN), so the shadow took the F8 fallback, the exit term alone or `NothingOpen`, and at an all-AMAN site its number was the exit term whenever a desk was open by its zones. Since ARV-117b (the owner's decision of the same day, for the shadow only) the shadow's desk term has the sensor cycle time below. Consequences: in a replay (no desk term) the shadow equals the published nowcast in every minute, and the replay ledger does not serialise it, so the golden replay is unchanged; where no AMAN input exists (a site without AMAN) the shadow equals the published nowcast in the minutes where it has no sensor cycle time, and differs where it has one, because the published nowcast never takes it (whether a site without AMAN should publish a sensor cycle time is a separate decision for the owner, docs/product/decisions.md). The shadow is never shown, never an alert input and never in a report or the live snapshot; only the validation comparison will read it: ARV-104f's pure engine compares the rows it is handed, and the validation results service reads them through the validation reader login, the only login that holds `ariva_validation_reader` (ARV-104g1, script 0049; `ShadowNowcastExposureTests`). It is computed always (no setting turns it off; with `Stream:DeskTermSeconds` 0 both nowcasts take the exit term alone).

Sensor cycle time of the shadow (ARV-117b, `SensorCycle`, Proposed values pending the owner, docs/product/decisions.md). Shadow only: the published nowcast, the live snapshot, screens, displays, alert inputs, reports and the AMAN contracts never take it.

```
c_sensor = (sum over the lane's desks and the W minutes of Serving seconds from desk_sensor_minute) / 60
           / (queue exits over the same W minutes)
mu_desk_shadow = n_open_sensor / c_sensor
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| W | Window in whole minutes ending at the desk term's minute (within the desk term's 15-minute read) | 10 (2 to 10) |
| X_min | Minimum queue exits in the window | 10 |
| c_min, c_max | Plausible cycle time per passenger at a desk | 0.2 min (12 s), 10 min |
| u_max | Largest share of the window's desk time that may be Unknown, missing or refused | 0.05 |
| N_max | Sensor-only desk minutes of one lane and window at most (CWE-120) | 4,000 |

Why Serving time and not open time. Open (Idle plus Serving) desk minutes over exits is the throughput identity again: with the same window as the exit term, n_open / c = (n_open / mean open desks in the window) x exit rate, so the desk term collapses into the exit term scaled by the change in open desks, and it carries the idle time of a short queue (the realised throughput, not the capacity). Serving time (staff present and the service zone occupied, F10 row 6, from the zones alone) over exits is the time a desk takes per passenger while it serves: the capacity per desk the desk term needs. What it adds over the exit term, and what it does not: it adds the desks open now (it reacts at once to a desk opening or closing) and the busy share of the open time (a short queue no longer lowers the throughput); under saturation, when every open desk serves all the time, it is still the exit rate over W per busy desk, because the sensors see no service starts: it measures no service speed of its own. Service-zone time excludes the walk-up gap, so where the gap is long against the service time c_sensor is shorter than the cycle time (D4) and the shadow optimistic; the simulator does not model the gap, so this needs pilot data. A desk's exits cannot be attributed to it (the queue's exit line is shared), so c is a lane mean.

Rules: the window holds the lane's desk minutes in [t_desk - W + 1, t_desk]; a sensor-only minute that is not a number, negative, above 60 seconds, busier than open, or whose open and Unknown time exceed a minute is left out, counted and treated as Unknown for the whole minute, as is a desk absent from a minute; above u_max Unknown there is no c (an Unknown desk may have served exits whose busy time is missing). A desk minute of the lane without a sensor-only minute (a desk without zones, or a minute written before ARV-117a) means no c; so does a minute of the window without any desk minute, exits not observed for every minute of the window, fewer than X_min exits, or c outside [c_min, c_max]. Without c the shadow falls back as F8 does: the exit term alone (flagged `Degraded` by `Nowcast.Compute`), or `NothingOpen` when the zones show every desk closed (c is not needed there, so the result is not flagged for it). A flagged or Unknown desk minute, a refused or missing one, or a degraded exit flags c and so the shadow. The value taken is stored with the shadow (`queue_minute_shadow.sensor_cycle_minutes`, script 0045, null when it fell back), which the runtime role writes but cannot read. Desk minutes read back from `desk_sensor_minute` are checked against script 0044's bounds before use (CWE-501): a row that fails is left out and counted, with a warning that names the site and the count only; a lane over N_max gives no c, with a warning that names the site and the count only.

## F9. Backlog recursion and capacity

Source: D4, D5. Per interval k (forecast step; 15 minutes in D4, 1 minute in the reference simulator):

```
D_k     = min(Q_k + A_k, C_k)                served
Q_{k+1} = max(0, Q_k + A_k - C_k)             backlog carried forward
mu_k    = C_k / delta
W_k     ~ (Q_k + A_k / 2) / mu_k              expected wait of an arrival in interval k
```

Capacity from staffed desks and cycle times:

```
C_k = sum over desks d of tau_{d,k} / c_{d,k}
    = n_k * delta / c_k                      (homogeneous desks staffed all interval)
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Q_k | Backlog at the start of interval k (Q_0 = measured queue length now) | passengers |
| A_k | Arrivals in interval k (forecast demand, F13, F14, plus reject inflow, F12) | passengers |
| C_k | Capacity in interval k | passengers |
| tau_{d,k} | Minutes desk d is staffed and not paused in interval k | min |
| c_{d,k} | Cycle time of desk d (lane category specific) | min |

The term A_k / 2 assumes arrivals spread evenly within the interval (Proposed refinement of D4's "roughly the backlog ahead of it divided by capacity"). The reference simulator steps per minute and reports the joining wait as (Q_{k+1} + 0.5) / mu_k, capped at 120 min. It also scales capacity by (1 - pause fraction) per process (reference: 0.035 immigration, 0.03 check-in, 0.02 security, 0 e-gates).

Edge cases: C_k = 0 makes W_k undefined; report the cap (reference 120 min) with a "no service" flag. Arrivals are fluid (non-integer) in forecasts.

The staffing recommendation's pilot version (F16, ARV-181a, lead decision 2026-10-15) keeps this recursion but judges an interval by its longest joining wait, max(Q_k, Q_(k+1)) / mu_k, not by W_k above, which leaves out what the desks serve during the interval; see F16.

Tests:

- C = 30 per 15 min, Q_0 = 0, A = 60, 60, 0: Q = 30, 60, 30; D = 30, 30, 30.
- C = 30 per 15 min, A = 30, 30, 30: Q stays 0.
- Q_k = 30, A_k = 60, C_k = 30 per 15 min (mu = 2 per min): W_k = (30 + 30) / 2 = 30 min.
- Capacity: 4 desks all interval, c = 1.5 min, delta = 15: C = 40. One desk opening 5 minutes into the interval contributes 10 / 1.5 = 6.67.

## F10. Desk state: precedence and staleness

Source: D4 (states, transitions, precedence, T1 and T2); staleness rule Proposed.

States: `Closed`, `Idle`, `Serving`, `Paused`, `Unknown`.

| Rank | Signal | Source | Proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Transaction start and end | AMAN; common-use check-in transactions | `Serving`, exactly |
| 2 | Login and logout | AMAN desk session (`DeskSessionChanged`); common-use agent sign-in | Staffed candidate; logout proves `Closed` |
| 3 | Staff zone occupied behind the desk | Sensor | Someone is present |
| 4 | Service zone occupied in front of the desk | Sensor | Probably serving; weak on its own |
| none | A passenger walking up | Sensor | Nothing (people approach closed desks) |

Evaluation at time t (first matching row wins):

| # | Condition | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every signal source for the desk is stale (no heartbeat or update within T_stale) | `Unknown` |
| 2 | Login source available and the latest session event is a logout or Closed | `Closed` |
| 3 | A transaction is in progress (start without end) | `Serving` |
| 4 | Logged in (or, without a login source, staff zone occupied) and the staff zone has been empty for at least T2 | `Closed` |
| 5 | Logged in (or staff present) and the staff zone has been empty for at least T1 with no transaction in that time. Where no staff-zone sensor exists, activity means transactions only | `Paused` |
| 6 | No login source, staff present, and the service zone occupied | `Serving` (weak; flagged as sensor-derived) |
| 7 | Logged in, or no login source and staff present | `Idle` |

Lane cycle time per person (ARV-117d, Proposed, the method accepted by the owner on 2026-10-07 with the walk-up gap to re-measure on pilot data, ARV-104; it replaced ARV-064's transaction-weighted cycle per transaction). AMAN's interval statistics count a transaction in the interval in which it completes, whole, with its cycle from the previous transaction's start at the desk (a transaction spanning a minute boundary is not spread over the minutes it spans; the simulator's AMAN emulator follows this since ARV-117c; to confirm with AMAN, pilot-to-confirm TC-84). Over the lane's desks' intervals that started in the exit window (and the two minutes they may arrive late by):

```
c_lane = sum over intervals i of (t_i x T_i) / sum over intervals i of D_i / 60        min per person
t_i    = MeanService_i                                                                  (built)
```

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| T_i | `TransactionsProcessed`: approaches completed (a family processed together is one) |
| D_i | `DocumentsProcessed`: travel documents, so people |
| MeanService_i | `MeanServiceSeconds`: transaction start to end, so no time waiting for a passenger |
| t_i x T_i | The interval's working seconds; over documents, seconds per person |

Why per person: F8's Q counts people, so its c must be per person; per transaction the desk term's throughput is low by the people per transaction (1.25 on the reference day: 20 percent). Why service time and not AMAN's `MeanCycleSeconds`: a start-to-start cycle while open includes a desk's idle time after a lull, which inflates c at the start of every wave (on the reference evening the per-transaction cycle read up to 23 minutes). The alternative, each interval's cycle capped at its P90 service, is biased low with one or two transactions an interval (its P90 is its own transaction's service, its cycle the previous one's), and floored at the mean service (t_i = max(MeanService_i, min(MeanCycle_i, max(P90_i, MeanService_i))), `LaneCycleMethod.CycleCappedAtP90`, kept as the alternative) it measured no better; decisions.md has the comparison. What the built method gives up is the walk-up gap (F8).

```
s = the lane's expected service time: c_lane of the queue zones the desk serves (lane cycle time per person above,
    ARV-117d: mean service time per document) over the AMAN intervals of every desk serving them that started in the

I = the interval one completed-transactions signal covers (contract V1: 1 min)
P = max(T1, min(k x s, T_tx))          (P = T1 when s is not known)
A = the latest of: the end of the last interval with a transaction, the session start, and (while people wait) the
    start of the waiting
```

| # | Condition (logged in, AMAN live, no staff or service zone) | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 5a | The last interval with a transaction ended less than min(s + I, P) ago: transactions keep arriving within s | `Serving` |
| 5b | Q >= 1 and t - A < P | `Serving` |
| 5c | Q >= 1 and t - A >= P | `Paused` |
| 5d | Q = 0: logged in with nobody waiting | `Idle` |
| 5e | Q not known and t - A >= P | `Paused` |

Parameters (Proposed for the KPI annex): k = 4 (`Border:DeskFeed:RhythmPauseMultiple`, 2 to 20); the minimum of P is T1 (3 min) and its maximum T_tx, the longest transaction (`MaxTransaction`, 30 min); I = 1 min (`CompletedInterval`, AMAN contract V1); W_s = 15 min (`Border:DeskFeed:RhythmWindowMinutes`, 5 to 60); Q at most 5 minutes old (as the desk term's freshness, F8); the lane context older than T_stale (2 min) counts as none. With s = 1.5 min (the reference Visitors lane): a rhythm window of 2.5 min and P = 6 min; with s = 0.5 min (Citizens): 1.5 min and P = T1 = 3 min.

Why these rows. s + I: a transaction is counted in the interval it ends in and reported at that interval's end, so at a desk serving one traveller after another every s the next interval with a transaction ends at most s + I after the last one. The queue: AMAN's completed transactions alone cannot tell a desk with nobody to serve from one that stopped serving; with nobody waiting the desk is `Idle` however long it is silent (the empty hall), with people waiting it is `Serving` (a long transaction, a family, or calling the next traveller) until a silence of P. P from s: a family of three with a slow document takes several times the mean service time per person, so a silence of one or two s with people waiting is no pause; k scales the pause with the lane (on the reference day a visitor's service takes three times a citizen's). The start of the waiting: a desk idle through a quiet spell has had no transaction for a long time when a queue forms, and has P to start before it counts as paused. P = T1 without s and rows 5e and 5f without Q keep rows 5 and 7 as they were wherever the context is missing.

## F11. Data-quality propagation

Source: D5 invariants and failure modes.

| Condition in the bin | Flag |
|---|---|
| All sensors healthy, all desks known, clocks within threshold | `Good` |
| A sensor degraded, a desk `Unknown`, clock drift corrected, AMAN feed stale with last-known values used, censored tracks above threshold, a final bin's track completion below threshold (ARV-114b) | `Degraded` |
| An entry or exit line without coverage for the bin, or no measurement at all | `Unknown` |

The worst flag of any input wins. Every interval result, nowcast and forecast carries its flag.

Track completion (ARV-114b, Proposed; `BinSettings.TrackCompletionDegradedBelow`, `HealthChecks.TrackCompletionBelow`): a bin whose track completion (F18: tracks with outcome Exited over tracks that entered in the bin) falls below 0.90 is `Degraded`. Rules: it is judged once the bin is final, so every track that entered is resolved (a provisional bin's open tracks are people still queuing, not lost, and would flag every bin while it fills); a bin without a track that entered has no rate and is never flagged by it (zones whose sensors do not track people); the rate is compared with F18's tolerance of 1e-9, so 18 of 20 against 0.90 is not below; a threshold of 0 never flags. The threshold is the pilot's track completion target of F18 (both Proposed, to agree in the KPI annex). The flag is part of the bin result, so it flows wherever a bin's flag already flows: `queue_bin.quality` (replays and recomputations apply the same rule, so a recomputed bin gets no revision for it alone, except a bin made final before ARV-114b whose completion is below the threshold, which a recomputation revises to `Degraded`) and every reader of the stored bins. Why it is `Degraded` is kept beside it: `BinResult.DegradedByCompletionOnly`, stored as `queue_bin.degraded_by_completion_only` (script 0051), is true when the completion rule alone made the bin `Degraded` (final, below the threshold, `Good` without it; never true otherwise, which the table checks). The validation comparison reads it and judges such a bin like a `Good` one in every criterion (F18), since setting it apart would set apart exactly the bins whose tracking failed; it believes the flag only when the same bin's deciding health row is final, counted tracks and fell below the threshold, and otherwise treats the bin as unusable (a writer gone wrong is never Good). The flag is one of the values a revision compares (`BinResult.SameValues`), so a recomputation that finds another cause as well (a late outage over the bin, with the same counts) writes a revision that clears it. It is derived by the same code from the same inputs as the quality but is not part of the replay's hashed record (adding it would change every bin's hashed JSON); unit and integration tests check it (each other cause leaves it false: an outage or Unknown mark, a degraded input, the exact-wait bound, a forced finalisation, censored entrants). The conservation residual and occupancy out of range do not change a flag (Proposed): they compare two measurements without saying which is wrong, so they raise alarms instead (F20). Tests (`HealthChecksTests`): 8 of 10 tracks exited in a final bin with nothing else wrong gives `Degraded`, 9 of 10 `Good`; the same bin while provisional (no track exited yet) stays `Good`; a threshold of 0 or 0.8 leaves 8 of 10 `Good`, 0.95 flags 9 of 10; a bin of anonymous people only stays `Good`; a bin restored from a snapshot before it is final is flagged the same. The locked replays are unchanged (every track of the reference evening completes: the golden head stays 2b047dfce6d587d26ac5f99e4dd9c0817627b3b63d21792caf4f0a1cc6abd05e over 3,694 outputs; the locked overflow case keeps its head too).

Desk minutes (ARV-116): a desk minute is `Degraded` when any of its time was Unknown, when a configured source was stale (a staff or service zone counts once heard), or when the latest reading of a live staff or service zone was flagged by the sensing pipeline; its sensor-derived seconds are the time its state came from the zones alone. A sensor-only desk minute (ARV-117a, `desk_sensor_minute`) follows the same rule with the zones as the only sources, so a stale AMAN feed never degrades it; in the shadow nowcast's desk term it flags the term as a published minute does (flagged, or Unknown for at least 30 seconds). The shadow's sensor cycle time (ARV-117b, F8) is flagged when any desk minute of its window is flagged, Unknown, refused or missing, or an exit is degraded, and is not taken at all when more than 5 percent (Proposed) of the window's desk time is Unknown, refused or missing.

Implementation of "a sensor degraded" in the stream (ARV-036, `Ariva.Core/Queueing/DeviceLiveness`, Proposed): the zone processor follows its commissioned devices from its own records, so a replay finds the same outages as the live run. Every sensing batch and every device health report (`ariva.device.health.v1`, keyed by zone since ARV-036) means the device was heard at its receive time; a health report may say it is offline. A device heard before and silent for longer than 180 seconds (`DeviceSilenceSeconds`, the same as `Devices:Health:HeartbeatTimeoutSeconds`), or reporting itself offline, is out from the minute it was last heard in until the start of the minute its first push after it covers (`DeviceOutage`, kept in `zone_outage`): the minute the push interval before that push's receive time falls in (ARV-148, the owner's decision of 2026-10-10 on finding F4 of the ARV-104j rehearsal; before, the outage ended at the minute the push arrived in, so the bin after it was Degraded too). The devices are observed once for each new receive time, before any record of it is applied, so a silence beyond the limit is an outage whichever record of that receive time comes first. While a device is out the zone's live minutes are `Degraded` (from the moment the silence passes the limit) and its bins are marked `Degraded` for the outage at each new receive time, through the minutes known to be out (up to the start of the minute the push interval before the receive clock falls in), and once more when it ends; a bin is final only once those minutes have passed its end, so only the first mark of an outage can find a bin already final, which then asks for a recomputation. A device silent for 24 hours (`DeviceForgetHours`) is treated as removed: its outage ends there and it no longer counts. On the reference evening S-17 last pushes for 18:19 (received 18:20) and next for 18:30 (received 18:31), so the Visitors zone's outage is 18:20 to 18:30, its 18:15 bin is `Degraded` and its 18:30 bin is judged.

## F12. E-gate reject coupling

Source: D4; lag and live form from the reference simulator.

```
mu_gate        = (g / c_gate) * (1 - r)
A_manual(t)    = A_own(t) + r * F_gate(t - lag)
r              = rejects / attempts             (AMAN EGateIntervalStats over a window)
```

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| g | E-gates in service (not closed, not out of service) |
| c_gate | Mean e-gate cycle time, min |
| r | Reject rate, 0 to 1 |
| F_gate | Live: passengers processed by the gates; planning: e-gate arrivals (D4's form) |
| lag | Walk from the gates to the manual queue (reference 1 min) |

Which manual lane receives rejects is a site rule (reference: the visitors lane), To confirm per site. Reference reject rate 0.07.

Tests: g = 6, c_gate = 0.3 min (18 s), r = 0.07: mu_gate = 18.6 per min. E-gate flow 100 in an interval with r = 0.07 adds 7 to the manual lane's arrivals.

Implementation (ARV-049, `Ariva.Core/Border/EgateCoupling`): r is measured from AMAN's e-gate intervals over the last `Border:EgateCoupling:RateWindowMinutes` (30) when they hold at least `MinAttempts` (20) attempts, else the reference 0.07; the rejects join `RejectLane` (VIS, one per deployment, To confirm per site) after `LagMinutes` (1). In the arrival projection that predicted-breach rules read (ARV-047), the reject lane's demand per minute is its own plus r times the e-gate eligible arrivals a lag earlier (planning form), and the first lag minutes take the rejects AMAN reported just before (live form); the e-gate lane keeps its arrivals, since rejected passengers queue at the gates first. AMAN's e-gate intervals also fill `egate_minute` (processed = attempts, rejected, mean cycle). The arrival-wave API shows r and the reject lane to callers with the lane split.

## F13. Departure demand and show-up curves

Source: D4.

```
A_p(t) = sum over flights f of  N_f * Pr(t <= STD_f - S_f < t + delta) * sigma_{p,f}
Pr(t <= STD_f - S_f < t + delta) = F_S(STD_f - t) - F_S(STD_f - t - delta)
```

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A_p(t) | Passengers reaching process p (check-in, security, emigration) in [t, t + delta) |
| N_f | Booked passengers, or seats x forecast load factor |
| S_f | Minutes before departure a passenger reaches process p; its distribution F_S is the show-up curve, learned per segment (destination region, time of day, weekday, season, airline) |
| sigma_{p,f} | Process share: online check-in skips desks; e-gate eligibility splits emigration lanes |

(F_S here is the cumulative distribution; take the endpoints so that each passenger is counted in exactly one interval.)

Learning show-up curves: anonymous sensors cannot tell which flight a passenger is on. Curves are learned from check-in or boarding-pass timestamps per flight where the airport shares them, or by fitting curves so that the summed flight demand reproduces measured entry counts. New or seasonal routes borrow curves from similar routes until they have history.

Reference show-up curve (simulator): arrivals between STD - 180 and STD - 45 min, minute weights proportional to x^1.6 * (1 - x)^1.3 with x = (k + 0.5) / 136 for k = 0 to 135, normalised; online check-in share 0.40 goes straight to security (3 min walk).

Edge cases: a flight with N_f = 0 contributes nothing; flights departing after the horizon still contribute to early intervals.

Test: N = 200, sigma = 0.6, S uniform on [45, 180] min, STD = 12:00, t = 10:00, delta = 15: S in (105, 120], Pr = 15 / 135 = 0.111, A = 13.33.

## F14. Arrival hall curve and arrival-wave alert

Source: D4 (on-block plus deplaning plus walking or bus time, spread over a distribution learned per stand type); the 8 to 15 minute delay and 12-minute spread are the reference values given in the build brief and used in the simulator.

```
d_f      in [8, 15] min           delay from on-block to first arrival at the hall (deplane plus walk or bus)
A_l(t)   = sum over flights f of  P_f * share_{l,f} * omega_{t - (T_f + d_f)}
omega_j  = (3, 6, 9, 11, 12, 12, 11, 10, 8, 7, 6, 5) / 100   for j = 0 to 11, else 0
T_f      = estimated in-block before landing, actual in-block after
```

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| P_f | Arriving passengers: API boarded count or the airline load message, transfers removed |
| share_{l,f} | Share for lane category l from API nationality and document type against eligibility rules (`InboundFlightLaneDemand` at AMAN sites) |
| omega | Reference 12-minute spread, weights sum to 1 |

Reference lane split (simulator, without API): with mix shares for CRW, CIT, RES, VIS, TRF and e-gate share e of eligible citizens and residents:

```
CRW = P * mix_CRW
CIT = P * mix_CIT * (1 - e)
RES = P * mix_RES * (1 - e)
VIS = P * mix_VIS
EG  = P * (mix_CIT + mix_RES) * e
transfers (mix_TRF) are removed
```

Reference values: mix CIT 0.35, RES 0.20, VIS 0.35, CRW 0.02, TRF 0.08; e = 0.40; expected delay 11 min (midpoint) when unknown.

Arrival-wave alert: the sum of A_l(t) over [now + 5, now + 25] min per hall or lane, compared with a threshold or with the capacity of staffed desks over the same window (threshold To confirm).

Predicted breach (ARV-038, rule metric `PredictedNowcast`, lead time L from 15 to 60 minutes): from the queue Q(t) and the throughput mu(t) of the minute (F8),

```
Q(t + k) = max(0, Q(t + k - 1) + A(t + k) - mu(t))     for k = 1 to L, with Q(t + 0) = Q(t)
W(t + k) = (Q(t + k) + 1) / mu(t)
predicted(t) = max over k of W(t + k), for the first k that reaches it
```

The rule judges predicted(t) against its threshold like a nowcast, and its alert names t + k as the minute the breach is projected for. Taking the highest W over the horizon, not only W(t + L), keeps a wave that the desks would have cleared by t + L. No value without mu(t) > 0 or without A for every minute of the horizon, and none while the queue length is degraded.

Tests (ARV-038):

- Q = 20, mu = 5, A = 10 per minute for L = 15: W peaks at k = 15, (20 + 75 + 1) / 5 = 19.2.
- Q = 0, mu = 10, a single minute of 100 arrivals at k = 3 and none after: W peaks at k = 3, (90 + 1) / 10 = 9.1.

Tests:

- P = 200, one lane, T = 18:00, d = 10: arrivals per minute from 18:10 to 18:21 are 6, 12, 18, 22, 24, 24, 22, 20, 16, 14, 12, 10 (sum 200).
- Lane split, P = 200 with the reference mix: CRW 4, CIT 42, RES 24, VIS 70, EG 44 (total 184; 16 transfers removed).

Implementation (ARV-047, `Ariva.Core/Flights/ArrivalWave`): T_f is the actual on-block; after a landing without one, the later of landing plus taxi-in (`Flights:ArrivalWave:TaxiInMinutes`, 5, Proposed) and the estimate; else the estimate (the AODB's estimated on-block); else the schedule; it is taken to the whole minute, so each passenger counts in exactly one minute. P_f and the split are AMAN's `InboundFlightLaneDemand` for the same site and flight key when received (P_f its boarded total, the lanes and e-gate eligible as sent, the rest taken as transfers), else the feed's passenger estimate, else seats times a load factor (0.8, Proposed), each with the default mix (`Flights:ArrivalWave:Mix`, the reference values above, To confirm per site); a flight with none of these is listed without passengers and counted. d_f is one value per deployment (`DelayMinutes`, 11, from 8 to 15) until stand types are known. The projection keeps arrivals neither cancelled nor diverted that land within the window (5 to 120 minutes, default 30; up to 195 minutes for the staffing recommendation's projection only, `ArrivalWave.MaxProjectionWindow`, ARV-181a, F16) or whose hall minutes are not all past, and gives hall arrivals per minute and lane from the current minute for window + d + 12 minutes. The alert window sum covers the 20 minutes from now + 5 to now + 25. For predicted-breach rules (`ProjectedArrivalWave`, ARV-038's arrival source), a queue zone receives the lanes of the desks its service zones stand at in the site's published profile, each lane shared equally among the queue zones that serve it (an overflow band takes its queue zone's); a zone serving no lane has no projection, and minutes before the current one have none, so a backtest finds none (at most its last minute when it ends now). The lane split is border data: airport roles get flight and minute totals only (`ArrivalWaveLanes.View` for the split).

Tests (ARV-047, `ArrivalWaveTests`): the two tests above; AMAN's split preferred to the mix; the in-block and passenger precedence; window edges, a flight still arriving and a flight fully arrived; floored in-block and the alert window.

## F15. Monte Carlo P50 and P90

Source: D4, D5; run count and perturbations from the reference simulator.

```
for r = 1..R:
    sample inputs (show-ups, on-blocks, loads, service times) with a deterministic seed
    run the backlog recursion (F9) from the current state
    record W_r(h) for each future step h, weighted by arrivals A_r(h)
P50(h) = v[floor(0.5 * (R - 1))]      v = W_1(h)..W_R(h) sorted ascending (0-based)
P90(h) = v[ceil(0.9 * (R - 1))]
```

Reference perturbations (simulator): R = 40 runs; horizon 120 min; flights not yet landed: on-block = estimated in-block + uniform(-6, +6) min; delay d = clamp(11 + round(uniform(-3, +3)), 8, 15); load x (1 + uniform(-0.04, +0.04)); service rate per queue divided by (1 + uniform(-0.08, +0.08)); joining wait capped at 120 min. Seed derived from (scenario seed, now, run index) so a run is reproducible.

For a 15-minute bin, combine per-step values across runs with arrival weights (F7), not by averaging step percentiles. Every run records the model version and an input snapshot.

Edge cases: R < 10 gives unstable P90 (Proposed minimum 20 in production). Zero capacity gives the cap.

Test: R = 40 with values 1, 2, ..., 40: P50 = v[19] = 20, P90 = v[36] = 37.

## F16. Staffing recommendation

Source: D4.

For each interval k choose the smallest n_k such that the predicted P90 wait (F15) stays at or below target, subject to maximum desks, minimum open durations, shift boundaries and break rules. Intervals are coupled through the backlog, so decide forward in time: fixing n_k changes Q_{k+1}. A single lane is solved greedily; a hall (shared officers across lanes) exactly with an integer optimiser (OR-Tools; v1).

Reference targets (simulator): P90 target 10 min for immigration and check-in, 8 min for security.

Test: with C per desk = 10 per 15 min, Q_0 = 0, A = 25, target "Q_{k+1} = 0": n = 3 (2 desks leave a backlog of 5).

### Pilot version: recommended open desks, indicative (ARV-181a)

Owner decision 2026-10-15 (docs/product/decisions.md): the pilot tells the supervisor how many desks to open, from what the MVP has, labelled indicative. Per arrivals-hall lane and 15-minute interval for the next 3 hours: the fewest open desks n_k that keep the predicted wait at or below the lane's target and each desk busy at most the cap's share of the interval with its arrivals, decided forward because n_k changes the backlog carried into k+1 (F9). Values marked Proposed are design proposals of the plan (2026-10-15), adjustable in configuration.

```
Q_(k+1)(n) = max(0, Q_k + A_k - n r delta_k)                    F9 with C_k = n r delta_k, mu_k = n r
W_k(n)     = max(Q_k, Q_(k+1)(n)) / (n r)                       n >= 1; W_k(0) = 0 when Q_k = A_k = 0, else no service
n_k        = the smallest n in [0, N] with W_k(n) <= T and A_k <= u n r delta_k

closed form (the engine's; a property test checks it against the search):
n_wait = max(1, ceil(Q_k / (r T)), ceil((Q_k + A_k) / (r (T + delta_k))))    while anyone waits or arrives; 0 otherwise
n_busy = ceil(A_k / (u r delta_k))
n*_k   = max(n_wait, n_busy);  n_k = min(n*_k, N), short by n*_k - N above it
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| k | Interval: k = 0 runs from the computed minute to the next quarter hour (1 to 15 minutes), k = 1 to 12 are the following quarter hours, so 13 rows reach the quarter hour after now plus 3 hours (decision of 2026-10-15). UTC quarter hours are local quarter hours in every time zone | |
| Q_0 | The lane queue's length in its latest live queue minute (F8's Q), while at most 3 minutes old (Proposed) and not degraded | passengers |
| A_k | The hall arrivals of the lane's category in interval k (F14, the e-gate rejects coupled as F12 does), divided equally among the arrivals-hall queue zones of that category | passengers |
| r | Throughput per open desk (below) | passengers per desk-minute |
| N | The lane's desks in service: kind Desk at the immigration and emigration checkpoints of the queue zone's level whose lane categories include the zone's (the nowcast desk term's match); a desk serving several lanes counts in each | desks |
| n_open | Desks open now: F8's n_open from the desks' latest closed minute (the desk-state count, Idle or Serving, F10), while at most 5 minutes old | desks |
| T | Target wait: `Staffing:TargetMinutes`, default 10 (the reference target for immigration above), with per-site and per-lane overrides | min |
| u | Busy cap per desk: `Staffing:BusyCap`, 0.85, from 0.5 to 1, per-site overrides (owner decision 2026-10-15) | share |

Why this predicted wait and not F9's (Q_k + A_k / 2) / mu_k (lead decision 2026-10-15): F9's form leaves out what the desks serve during the interval, so it overstates the fluid mean wait by delta / 2 = 7.5 min whenever the queue does not empty, and gives a wait even when capacity exceeds arrivals (Q 0, A 25, C 30: 6.25 min where nobody waits in the fluid model); and F16's own test above (target "Q_(k+1) = 0") cannot be written with it, while with W_k it is exactly T = 0. W_k is the longest joining wait of the interval in the fluid model (the backlog is piecewise linear, so the longest is at one end): the start term makes the backlog carried in clear within the target at this interval's desks, the end term covers its growth. It is the simulator's joining wait (Q_(k+1) + 0.5) / mu_k and F8's (Q + 1) / mu without the +1 (F9 is a fluid model; the +1 would make T = 0 unreachable and adds about c / n). W_k never rises with more desks and never falls with a longer queue or more arrivals, so "the fewest desks" is well defined.

The busy cap is measured on arrivals (owner decision, reading confirmed by the lead on 2026-10-15): the interval's arrivals fit within the cap's share of the recommended desks' capacity, a margin for the variability the deterministic recursion leaves out until v1's P90 (F15); the reference simulator's recommender has the same rule. The backlog stays the wait's business: while a predicted backlog is cleared the desks are fully busy by design. The cap can call for desks for a single heavy interval that the wait target alone would absorb as a queue (case 5 below: 19 desks at 0.85 against 11 for the wait alone). Every interval records what set its number (`Wait`, `Busy` or `Both`; `None` when no desk is needed).

Throughput per open desk (Proposed values):

```
r = (sum of X_m) / (sum of O_m) over the busy minutes m of the 60 whole minutes ending at the lane queue's latest live minute
```

X_m is the lane queue's exits in minute m, O_m its desks' open (Idle plus Serving) desk-minutes. A minute is used when its queue minute has a length that is not degraded, every desk of the lane heard in the window has its minute (none degraded, none Unknown for half of it, each within the bounds of script 0018; a desk never heard in the window, out of service or without a signal source, cannot have been open and is not waited for), some desk was open, and it was busy: queue length at least ceil(O_m), a person waiting for each open desk. Open time over exits otherwise measures the realised throughput, not the capacity (the throughput identity of F8's sensor cycle note). Measured when the busy minutes hold at least 30 open desk-minutes and 20 exits and 1 / r lies within 0.2 to 10 minutes; otherwise the cycle time configured for the lane's category at commissioning (`Staffing:Sites:<site>:FallbackCycleSeconds:<lane>`, 3 to 600 s; r = 60 / it); none is shipped (decision of 2026-10-15), so a lane without either has no recommendation. The desk-state agreement at the throughput level (D6, F18, ARV-149a) judges exactly the open against not open split O_m needs.

No guess (owner decision 2026-10-15). Every interval without a recommendation carries its reason: `NoQueueLength` (no fresh live queue minute, or a degraded length, or a length outside 0 to 1,000,000), `NoThroughput` (neither measured nor configured), `NoDesks` (no desk in service), each for the whole lane with its arrivals still given; `FlightWithoutPassengers` for every interval holding one of the hall minutes of a flight with no passenger figure (an SSIM leg without AMAN's lane demand, or an AODB leg without a passenger estimate or seats; F14 lists it without passengers), and `BacklogUnknown` for every interval after one whose arrivals are not known (the backlog carried into it would be a guess); `ArrivalsTruncated` when the site has more arriving legs than are read (2,000); `NoLaneDemand` for a lane category the arrival wave has no demand for (it knows CIT, RES, VIS and CRW at manual desks; e-gates are not desks); `ArrivalsOutOfRange` for an interval whose arrivals are not a finite number of at most 1,000,000 (a minute of the wave not finite or negative, or a sum above it), with `BacklogUnknown` after it. A value out of its range stays with its lane or interval: it never stops the site's other lanes (CWE-501). The departures hall has no recommendation in this version: its demand needs F13's show-up curves (v1).

## F17. SLA evaluation per bin, exclusions and allowance

Source: D5 (contracts, final bins only, profile version, evidence packs); exact rules and reference values from the prototype's SLA screen.

Per contract, per zone in scope, per final bin b of the contract's bin size:

```
evaluated_b = status_b == Final and pax_b >= minPax and value_b != null
breach_b    = value_b > threshold        (percentile wait, overflow minutes)
            = value_b < target_share     (share of passengers under the threshold)
excluded_b  = an exclusion of a type the contract allows overlaps b
              (exclusion.from < b + delta and exclusion.to > b),
              or the bin's dispute is Upheld
held_b      = the bin's dispute is Raised or UnderReview
```

Per evaluation window (calendar month or week):

```
breaches  = count(evaluated_b and breach_b)
counted   = breaches - count(breach_b and excluded_b) - count(breach_b and held_b and not excluded_b)
penalised = max(0, counted - allowance)
penalty   = min(cap, penalised * rate_per_bin)
```

KPI types (prototype): percentile wait per bin (default P90, arrival-weighted, F7), share of passengers under the threshold, overflow minutes per bin.

Overflow minutes per bin (ARV-115; the counting rule TC-19 is decided: accepted by the owner, Ahmad Al-Sadder, on 2026-10-06): a minute of a queue zone is an overflow minute when any of its overflow bands held anyone in it, that is when the highest reading of a band in that minute is above zero. A minute without a band reading is not observed (unknown, never zero). Implementation (`Ariva.Core/Queueing/OverflowBands`, script 0039): the queue engine already takes each band's occupancy readings (they count in the queue length, F2); the zone keeps each band's lowest and highest reading per minute until the watermark has passed the minute's end, then writes it to `overflow_minute` in the checkpoint transaction, so a reading late but inside the lateness allowance still counts and a written minute never changes (a recomputation is the only route to other numbers). `overflow_bin_15m` counts, per zone and 15-minute bin from UTC midnight, the overflow minutes, the minutes observed and the most people one band held. Each closed minute also moves the band's state: its first minute with occupancy after an empty or unheard spell raises `OverflowDetected` (Occupied, with the minute's peak), its first minute with readings and no occupancy after an occupied spell raises `OverflowDetected` (Emptied, with the spell's first minute and peak); so each change is reported once, at minute resolution, whatever the readings do within a minute. Silent band (decided by the owner on 2026-10-06, "Unknown after timeout"): a minute without readings keeps the band's state while its readings are fresh; a band occupied or empty whose latest minute with readings is L becomes Unknown at minute U, the first minute that starts after every reading of minute L is stale (L plus one minute plus the occupancy freshness window of the queue length, 2 minutes, rounded up to a whole minute: U = L + 3), when no reading arrived in minutes L + 1 to U. It is decided when the watermark passes U's end, so it is pure and replays the same, and it raises `OverflowDetected` (Unknown, with the spell's first minute and peak when the band was occupied, else 0). A reading in any minute up to U keeps the state, so a sensor that reports at least every third minute never flaps. The band's next minute with readings raises Occupied, or Emptied with a peak of 0, again. A band never heard is not Unknown (it has no state to lose); the end of a replay's range is not a silence (no band becomes Unknown while a replay settles past it, as no device outage is reported there). Unknown is neither occupied nor empty: an Unknown band has no `overflow_minute` rows, so it adds nothing to the overflow minutes or the minutes observed, and `OverflowOccupied` (alert rules) takes a value per minute from the watched bands with a reading in it only (1 when any of them held anyone, 0 when they held no one, no value when none reported; a minute without a value neither counts towards nor breaks a rule's sustain or clear run). Flagging a zone's health for a dead band sensor is not part of this rule (deferred). A band starts empty; a snapshot written before ARV-115 restores every band empty, and a version 5 snapshot (before the Unknown state) restores its bands occupied or empty as written. Bounds (CWE-120): only the bands of the published geometry are kept, at most 5,000 band minutes open per zone (in practice only the watermark's own minute is open); beyond it the earliest is released early, and a later part of that minute merges into the row (lowest of the lows, highest of the highs, the same row in any order) and may only raise Occupied for a band still empty. A restored snapshot is checked (bands of the geometry, aligned plausible minutes, readings from 0 to 10,000, a consistent spell, an Unknown band with neither spell nor peak and a minute it was last heard in) and refused otherwise (CWE-501). The stream's store drops, with one warning per zone that names no band, a band minute or change the tables or the outbox would refuse (an empty or overlong band name, readings out of order or above 10,000, a zone key without a site, or a zone key longer than the outbox's 200-character message key, whose change is dropped while its minutes are still written), so a checkpoint never fails on them.

Tests (OverflowBandsTests, OverflowReplayTests): readings 4, 9 and 2 in one minute give 2 and 9, occupied; a band reading 0, 3, 8, 6, 0 and 0 in minutes 0 to 5, then 2 and 0 within minute 6, then 0 in minute 7, raises Occupied at minute 1 (peak 3), Emptied at minute 4 (since minute 1, peak 8), Occupied at minute 6 and Emptied at minute 7, with 4 overflow minutes; a silent minute within the freshness window changes nothing. A band occupied in minutes 0 and 1 (peak 9) and silent after it is Unknown at minute 4, decided once the watermark passes 18:05 and not a tick before; an empty band last heard in minute 0 is Unknown at minute 3 and Emptied (peak 0) at its next reading; a sensor reporting every third minute never goes Unknown; one engine step or many give the same changes in the same order. The overflow scenario case (seed 9303 with the Visitors snake holding 60 people) fills A-OV from 18:03 to 18:31 and from 19:33 to 19:52: 49 overflow minutes (18:00 bin 12, 18:15 bin 15, 18:30 bin 2, 19:30 bin 12, 19:45 bin 8).

Overflow minutes as a penalty KPI (Proposed, product owner request 2026-10-07; To confirm per contract, TC-78 to TC-82):

- Penalised party: the ground handler that operates the zone's counters, not the airline. The handler is the party the airport contracts for check-in and controls counter staffing; one handler often serves several airlines on one island. Reports list the flights checking in during each breached bin so the handler can settle with its airlines under its own contracts.
- Scope: a contract names the queue zones it covers. Zones at border checkpoints (immigration, emigration, e-gates) are never in a penalty contract: they are reported and alerted only, since the border authority staffs them and per-desk border data stays in the border deployment. Accountability there belongs in an agreement between the airport and the authority.
- Attribution of a breached bin b of zone z: the parties are the distinct `handler_code` values of the counter allocations (ARV-041, `counter_allocation`) whose counters are desks served by z and whose open and close times overlap b. Exactly one handler: the bin is that handler's. Two or more handlers: not attributed automatically; the bin goes to manual review and is reported, never split by counters or passengers. Any allocation for z in b with an unresolved counter code or no handler code: not attributable; review. No allocation at all: not attributable; review.
- Observation floor: a bin is evaluated for overflow minutes only when its observed minutes (minutes with at least one band reading, `overflow_bin_15m`) reach the contract's minimum, Proposed 12 of 15. Below it the bin counts as a sensor outage (excluded and reported), never as a clean bin, so a band sensor that stops reporting cannot hide a breach.
- Staffed to plan: a further exclusion type, `StaffedToPlan`. A breached bin is excluded when the handler had every counter of the agreed staffing plan for the zone open during the bin, so a peak in the flight schedule is not charged to a handler that staffed as agreed. Needs the agreed plan per zone and bin (input To confirm).
- What a breach is: overflow minutes of the 15-minute bin above the contract's threshold, as for the other KPI types. A long overflow is several breached bins (40 minutes is about 3); no separate rule on consecutive minutes.

Tests (Proposed): bin with 9 overflow minutes of 15 observed, threshold 5, one handler: breach attributed to that handler. Same bin with 11 minutes observed and an observation floor of 12: excluded as a sensor outage. Same bin with counters of two handlers allocated: review, not counted. Same bin with one allocation holding an unresolved counter code: review, not counted. A breached bin with every planned counter open: excluded as `StaffedToPlan`.

Exclusion types (prototype; per contract, To confirm): security directive; sensor outage over the zone for more than 5 minutes in the bin (for overflow minutes, also a bin below the observation floor); counters closed on airport instruction; flight disruption outside the handler's control; staffed to plan (Proposed, overflow minutes); upheld dispute.

Reference contract values (prototype, not contractual): P90 per 15-minute bin at most 15 min; minimum 1 passenger per bin; allowance 6 breached bins a month; USD 350 per further breached bin; cap USD 10,000 a month; dispute window 5 working days.

Rules: provisional breaches are shown but never counted. Disputes apply to final breached bins only. When a dispute resolves, the evaluation is recomputed as a new revision. Every evaluation records contract version, zone profile version per bin and the evidence pack hash.

Tests:

- 10 breaches, 2 excluded, 1 held, allowance 6, rate 350: counted 7, penalised 1, penalty USD 350.
- 40 counted, allowance 6, rate 350, cap 10,000: penalised 34, 34 x 350 = 11,900, penalty USD 10,000.
- A sensor outage of 4 minutes within a breached bin does not exclude it; 6 minutes does.

## F18. Health checks and validation metrics

Source: D4 (checks, protocol, proposed acceptance targets); D6 (additional pilot criteria). Acceptance targets are proposals to agree contractually, not industry standards.

Continuous health checks:

```
conservation residual  r = (entries - exits) - (Occ(t + delta) - Occ(t))        should be 0
track completion rate  = tracks with outcome Exited / tracks that entered
occupancy sanity       0 <= Occ(t) <= zone physical capacity
```

Validation metrics per 15-minute bin:

```
count accuracy   = 1 - |N_system - N_manual| / N_manual                 per line
wait error       = w_system - w_tracer                                  per tracer
wait tolerance   |wait error| <= max(1 min, 0.10 * w_tracer)
bias             = sum(w_system - w_tracer) / sum(w_tracer)
```

| Criterion | Target |
|---|---|
| Count accuracy per 15-minute bin, each line | at least 95% |
| Realised-wait absolute error | within max(1 min, 10% of the true wait) |
| Realised-wait bias | within plus or minus 5% |
| Track completion | at least 90% |
| Desk-state agreement with observer log (D6) | at least 95% of observed minutes, each judged at its desk's level (ARV-149a): the full state for a desk with a staff or service zone, open or not open for a desk without one |
| Nowcast error against later realised wait (D6) | median within 2 min for waits under 20 min |
| Availability during the pilot (D6) | 99% of operating hours |
| Ground-truth proof (D6) | nowcast error with and without AMAN inputs, side by side; no target |

Re-validate after any layout change, sensor replacement or health-check alarm, and at least quarterly for penalty-grade zones. Validation runs at least five operating days including two peak days (assumption).

Edge cases: N_manual = 0 makes count accuracy undefined; report absolute error instead.

Tests: N_system = 96, N_manual = 100 gives 96%. Tracer 8 min, system 8.9 min: tolerance 1 min, pass. Tracer 20 min, system 22.5 min: tolerance 2 min, fail.

Tests of the continuous checks (ARV-114a, `HealthChecksTests`): entries 30, exits 25, Occ 10 then 15 gives r = 0; Occ 10 then 12 gives r = 3 (counted in, not seen in the occupancy); Occ 10 then 18 gives r = -3; a bin with no entries or exits and Occ 4 then 4 gives r = 0, Occ 4 then 6 gives r = -2; no occupancy at either end gives no residual. 20 tracks entered and 18 exited gives a completion rate of 0.9; no track entered gives no rate (neither 0 nor 1). With a capacity of 40 a minute whose readings reach 41 is outside, 40 is not; a reading below 0 is outside with or without a capacity.

Implementation of the continuous checks (ARV-114a, `Ariva.Core/Queueing/HealthChecks`, script 0038), per queue zone and bin, written to `zone_health_bin` with every bin result (same start, revision and status; a Final row never changes, a recomputation adds a revision):

- Conservation residual: entries and exits are the bin's own (as in `queue_bin`); Occ(t) is the queue's sensor occupancy at the bin boundary t: the latest reading of the queue zone plus the latest readings of the overflow bands heard so far, each at or before t and fresh there (within the occupancy freshness of the queue length, 2 minutes). A band never heard counts as empty, so people standing in a band without a sensor show in the residual. The engine samples this at every minute boundary its watermark passes (no sample when a reading is stale, and none when the queue zone has never reported); the residual is null when either end of the bin has no sample, for example the first bin of a zone that starts with no reading before it.
- Track completion: tracks are people who entered with a track of their own (T3); the rate is tracks with outcome Exited over tracks that entered in the bin, null when none entered. Tracks abandoned, fragmented (lost, or dropped when the queue was observed empty), censored, rejected (an exit before the entry) and still open are reported apart; while the bin is provisional its open tracks lower the rate.
- Occupancy sanity: per minute, each zone's lowest and highest reading is checked against that zone's physical capacity (queue zones and overflow bands, optional, 1 to 5,000, set in the draft profile, not part of the geometry hash). A minute counts as observed when any zone of the queue reported, as checked when a reported zone has a capacity, and as outside when a reading was below 0 or above its zone's capacity. Readings below 0 are refused at Ingest and again by the stream (CWE-501), so in practice only the capacity can be exceeded.

Thresholds (residual 0, completion at least 90%) are Proposed. Since ARV-114b a final bin whose completion is below 90 percent is `Degraded` (F11), and alert rules judge each check per queue zone and bin (`ConservationResidual`, `TrackCompletion`, `OccupancyOutOfRange`, F20). The reference evening (golden replay, seed 9303) conserves people in every bin with both ends measured, completes every track and stays within the snake capacities (A-VIS 190, CI-C 70), because the sensor emulator's occupancy is exactly its entries minus its exits; the replay with a capacity of 40 for CI-C has minutes outside it in the 19:15 and 19:30 bins. The health of the 18:00 A-VIS bin: entries 243, exits 108, Occ 10 then 145, r = 0, 243 tracks entered and exited, 15 minutes observed and checked, none outside.

Implementation of N_system per line (ARV-113, `Ariva.Core/Queueing/LineCounts`, script 0037): the queue engine counts every crossing of a line of the zone (entry, exit, overflow entry and count lines) in each direction as it applies it, and spreads interval counts over their minutes as it spreads them for the queue; a count line never moves the queue. A line minute is closed, and written to `line_minute` with source `Ariva`, once the watermark has passed its end: an event that arrives late but still ahead of the watermark (inside the lateness allowance) revises the open minute, and an event behind the watermark is late and not applied (F5, F6), so a written line minute never changes except through a recomputation. Bound: a zone holds at most 20,000 open line minutes (`LineCounts.MaxOpenLineMinutes`; in practice only the lines of the watermark's own minute are open, because the engine applies events only up to its watermark). Beyond the bound the earliest is released early, before the watermark has passed it; every minute up to the latest one released early (the mark, kept in the zone snapshot until the watermark passes it) is then written as additive parts (`LineMinute.Additive`): parts in one checkpoint are merged, and the upsert adds a part to the row already written instead of replacing it, so no count is lost and the row reaches its full count once the watermark passes the minute. Such a minute grows while the watermark has not passed it; the zone's outputs and its snapshot commit in one transaction, so each part is added once, with one exception: if a commit succeeds on the server but reports an error to the client and the same process retries it, an additive part is added twice. Additive parts need more than 20,000 open line minutes, which the engine never holds (only the watermark's own minute is open), so this needs a corrupt or tampered snapshot. `line_minute_15m` sums them per 15-minute bin for the comparison with manual counts. Vendor-computed crossings are a separate source (`Vendor`), never added to Ariva's counts; they are stored once Ariva computes crossings from tracks (ADR-0003), because until then the engine's crossings are the ones the devices send.

Capture of N_manual (ARV-104a, script 0047; the rules marked Proposed are in docs/product/decisions.md): a validation campaign binds a site, one published zone profile version (number and geometry hash), queue zones of that version and the lines the queue engine counts for them (entry, exit, overflow entry and count lines of a queue zone in scope or of one of its overflow bands, so every line has `line_minute` rows under its queue zone's key and the version), and 1 to 31 planned local days in the site's time zone. An observer (an account holding the Validation observer role that neither created nor started the campaign) records, per line and 15-minute bin (UTC, on the quarter hour, as `line_minute_15m` buckets them), the people crossing in and out (0 to 10,000 each). There is one count per line, bin and observer; a correction is a new revision with a reason that names the revision it corrects, so the observer's N_manual for a line and bin is its latest revision and every earlier one stays. A bin is accepted only when it starts on a planned local day, has ended (one minute of clock tolerance) and ended before the version was retired, so each count compares with `line_minute_15m` rows of the campaign's version alone. Both directions are stored; which direction a line's accuracy compares (crossings in for an entry line, out for an exit line, or both) and how two observers of one line and bin combine (Proposed: the mean of their current counts, with their difference reported) are rules of the comparison engine (ARV-104e).

Capture of tracer runs and desk observations (ARV-104b, script 0048; the rules marked Proposed are in docs/product/decisions.md). Same campaign rules as counts: the campaign runs, the observer neither created nor started it, the time is on a planned local day, has ended (one minute of clock tolerance) and ended before the version was retired.

- Tracer runs (w_tracer): a tracer is a campaign label (`T-07`), never a name. The capturing device sends its own clock reading `deviceClockUtc` with each batch (read again at every retry); Ariva measures, at receipt and to the millisecond, `offset = deviceClockUtc - receivedUtc` (F19's sign: positive when the device runs ahead; the network delay, a few hundred milliseconds at most, is part of it) and stores every run's device times, the offset and the corrected times `t_server = t_device - offset`. A batch whose |offset| exceeds 5 minutes is refused (Proposed): correcting a device that far off would hide a wrong setting. A run lasts more than 0 and at most 3 hours (Proposed: T_censor of F6 and an hour), its join is on a planned local day (the wait belongs to the entry interval, F6), its exit not in the future. Before the correction, each device time must lie between 32 days before and one minute after the batch's `deviceClockUtc` (`t_device - deviceClockUtc = t_server - receivedUtc`, so the upper bound is the same one-minute tolerance; beyond it the answer is the future-time rule, before it the planned-day rule): a well-formed but absurd time (year 1, year 9999) is refused instead of overflowing the correction. An observer records at most 500 runs per campaign (Proposed); a run already recorded is the same tracer code and device join time. `w_tracer = exited - joined` (corrected; an offset applied to both ends leaves the wait unchanged, it moves only which entry minute the run is compared with). Abandoned runs (the tracer left without being served) have no realised wait to compare (F5 excludes abandoned tracks): Proposed for ARV-104e, they are left out of the wait error and the bias and reported apart with their count and their zone's abandonment outcomes over the same interval, with no target.
- Desk observations: one observer's state (Closed, Idle, Serving or Paused; F10's states a person can see, never Unknown) of a staffed border desk per UTC minute, sent as one batch per 15-minute bin (minutes that have ended; a part bin is allowed) and corrected as a new revision with a reason. Border data: recorded and read back only by a Validation observer with no other role or by an account with `BorderDesks.View` (an account with an airport role and the observer role gets 403, docs/domain/data-boundary.md). The desk-state agreement of ARV-104f compares each observer's latest revision for a desk and minute with that minute of `desk_minute` (the dominant state); how two observers of the same desk and minute count is ARV-104f's rule (below, after the comparison engine's tests).

Tests (`GroundTruthTests`): a device 42.5 s ahead that read 05:10:42.500 and 05:28:12.500 gives a run of 05:10:00 to 05:27:30 on the server and a wait of 17.5 minutes; an offset of exactly 5 minutes is accepted and 5 minutes and 1 ms refused; an exit 60 s ahead of the server's clock is accepted and 61 s refused; a run of exactly 3 hours is accepted and one a second longer refused; at 06:00 the minute 06:00 (ending 06:01) is accepted and 06:01 refused; device times of year 1 (device ahead) and year 9999 (device behind) are refused without an exception, an exit exactly a minute after the device's clock reading passes and a millisecond later is refused, a join exactly 32 days before it passes the window; 499 runs and one more are accepted and the next refused.

Comparison engine (ARV-104e, `Ariva.Core/Validation/Comparison`, `ValidationComparison.Compare`; the rules marked Proposed are in docs/product/decisions.md). Pure: no I/O, no persistence, no clock. Every result carries the profile version.

- Standing of an item (a line's bin and direction, a tracer run, a zone's bin), Proposed: the item is compared only when the stored results over its time are Good: every 15-minute bin the time overlaps has a stored bin of the campaign's version, final where the item needs it, of quality Good, and no quality interval overlaps the time. Otherwise it is reported apart, with its count, as the later of NoSystemWait, Degraded, Unknown (a bin without a stored result, or whose latest revision cannot be used, is Unknown), Provisional and OtherVersion. Values are still shown wherever a stored result exists; only Good items enter a criterion. The continuous checks of `zone_health_bin` (residual, minutes outside capacity) do not change a standing (their alarms are alert rules, ARV-114b). A final bin that F11 marks `Degraded` for its own track completion alone (ARV-114b, `queue_bin.degraded_by_completion_only`) stands `Good` for every criterion (Proposed, the lead's fail-closed decision of the ARV-114b security review): setting it apart would set apart exactly the bins whose tracking failed and could turn a failing line, tracer or nowcast into a pass (ten bins at 99 percent beside one at 80 percent with 15 of 20 tracks exited fail count accuracy as before ARV-114b). A bin `Degraded` for another cause as well, or under an outage, Unknown, Provisional or of another version, is still set apart. A comparison recomputed after ARV-114b (a new results revision: Validation.Manage with a second factor, audited) applies the rule; results frozen before keep theirs. Stored bins are F18's 15-minute UTC bins on the quarter hour (a site whose bins are offset from the quarter hour, `BinSettings.DayStartOffset` not a multiple of 15 minutes, has no bins to compare).
- Count accuracy, per line, bin and direction. Directions (Proposed): crossings in for an entry or overflow entry line, crossings out for an exit line, both for a count line (two items); people turning back over an entry or exit line are not judged. N_manual is the mean of the observers' latest counts, with their largest difference reported (Proposed in ARV-104a); a line and bin where an observer's latest revision is refused or conflicting is not judged at all (no earlier revision stands in, and the other observers' counts of it are left out with it, since their mean would silently lose that observer). N_system is the line's Ariva crossings of the campaign's version in the bin (a vendor's crossings are never N_system; a line without a row in a Good bin crossed 0 times; a row of the campaign's version that is refused or conflicting leaves N_system unknown and the item Unknown, never 0; a bin also counted under another version, whatever that row holds, is OtherVersion). `accuracy = max(0, 1 - |N_system - N_manual| / N_manual)`: the floor at 0 is Proposed (F18's form falls below 0 when the system counts more than twice the manual count), and |N_system - N_manual| is always reported; with N_manual = 0 there is no accuracy and no verdict, only the absolute error. Per line: the Good items judged, those at or above the target, the bins judged (Good, with a judged item; a count line's two directions are one bin) and excluded (not Good, or not judged for an unusable count), the lowest and the pooled accuracy `1 - sum |N_system - N_manual| / sum N_manual`, and the verdict (Proposed reading of "per 15-minute bin, each line"): every judged item at or above the target (default 0.95); no judged item is no data.
- Tracer matching rule (Proposed): a tracer who joined queue zone z at the corrected time t is compared with Ariva's final realised wait of the people who entered z at t, read from `queue_minute`: each minute's mean realised wait (F5 to F7, of the people who entered in that minute) stands at the minute's centre, and w_system(t) is the straight line between the two centres around t. A neighbour minute that is not final, of another version or without waits, or whose stored results are not Good and final (its 15-minute bin stored, of the campaign's version, final and Good, and no quality interval over the minute), is not used, and on that side the join minute's own mean holds: revised after the security review of ARV-104e, where a neighbour in the previous bin, or before the join under an outage, weighed up to half while the run stayed Good. The join minute must be final, of the campaign's version and hold at least one realised wait; otherwise the run is Provisional, OtherVersion or NoSystemWait (Ariva has no wait of anyone who entered then; reported apart, since the count accuracy judges missed entries), and Unknown when its row is refused or conflicting. The run's standing covers its whole wait [joined, exited) and the minutes read: the join minute whole, from its start (its mean includes people who entered before the tracer), and the neighbour, read only when Good. A device outage while the tracer waited, or in the join minute before the join, makes it Degraded. A neighbour minute whose row is refused or conflicting is treated as absent, as one without a row: the join minute's mean holds on that side, the run stays Good (the neighbour was not read) and the minute's key is listed unusable. Finality (Proposed, decisions point 13): the join minute's row must be Final, but the 15-minute bins over the join minute and the tracer's wait are read for their version and quality only, not their status (a run over a Provisional queue bin can be Good: its join minute's realised waits are final), while a neighbour's bin must also be Final. Why not the cumulative curves: rebuilding A(t) and D(t) from the minutes' entries and exits (F5's FIFO form) re-derives a wait Ariva does not publish (at T3 sites the published waits are per track), needs an anchor (an empty queue or the queue length) and lets count drift (abandonment, count errors) accumulate into the wait, so the wait criterion would partly repeat the count criterion; the stored minute means are Ariva's own final realised waits, the numbers SLA evaluation reads.
- Wait error and bias: `e = w_system - w_tracer`, within tolerance when `|e| <= max(1 min, 0.10 x w_tracer)` (compared to 1e-9 minutes); `bias = sum(e) / sum(w_tracer)` over the Good compared runs, per zone and over all zones, so its sign is the sign of the errors' sum; per zone also the mean and the largest error. Verdicts per zone: the wait error passes when the share of compared runs within tolerance reaches the target (Proposed default 1.0, every run, the literal reading of the criterion; the KPI annex may set a share), the bias when |bias| <= 0.05. Abandoned runs (owner decision 2026-10-08, Proposed for the KPI annex) have no error and no part in the bias: they are counted per zone and set beside the system's abandoned entrants and entries in the final bins of the campaign's version their joins fall in, with no target. Tracer runs are never corrected.
- Clock offsets (ARV-104b security review): per observer (a pseudonymous Ariva user id, never a name), the spread of its batches' offsets, one value per batch: minimum, median (of an even count, the mean of the two middle values) and maximum. A batch whose offset differs from its observer's median by more than 5 seconds (Proposed) is flagged; its runs stay in the comparison, flagged. Sensitivity (Proposed shift 2 seconds): every compared run is matched again with every offset lowered and raised by the shift (`t_server = t_device - offset`, so a raised offset moves the corrected join and exit earlier and leaves w_tracer unchanged), giving its error at both; a shifted join reads by the same rule and gives no error when the minutes it reads are not Good. Per zone the bias and the runs within tolerance at minus the shift, as measured and plus the shift, over the runs whose system wait is known at all three, and the largest change of one run's error.
- Track completion per zone: over every 15-minute bin of the planned days, the latest `zone_health_bin` revision; the rate is the tracks exited over the tracks entered in the Good bins, pooled (never an average of bin rates), target 0.90. Since ARV-114b F11 flags a final bin below 0.90 as `Degraded`, which alone would leave exactly the failing bins out of this criterion; so a final bin of the campaign's version whose own completion is below the target is judged whatever its `queue_bin` flag says (Proposed): a bin `Degraded` for its completion alone is judged by every criterion anyway (above), and one `Degraded` for another cause as well lies below the target too, so judging it can only lower the pooled rate (fail closed). Quality intervals (outages, uncovered lines), Unknown bins, other versions and provisional bins still set a bin apart. Test: Good 18 of 20 beside a Degraded 7 of 10 gives 25 of 30, a fail (18 of 20 alone would pass); a Degraded 10 of 10 and a 5 of 10 under an outage are counted apart. Degraded and Unknown bins (a bin of the planned days without a stored row, or whose latest revision cannot be used, is Unknown) are reported apart with their tracks; Provisional and OtherVersion bins are counted, and every bin that is not Good is counted as excluded. No stored output is missing: `zone_health_bin` holds each bin's tracks since ARV-114a, and the bin's quality comes from `queue_bin`. Only zones whose sensors track people (T3) have tracks; elsewhere there is no rate (no data, never 0 or 1).
- Verdicts and sample size: the verdicts per line and per zone judge only the items they hold (one Good bin at 99 of 100 beside nine bins under an outage passes a line). They are not acceptance verdicts on their own: the campaign verdict (ARV-104g) requires the campaign's targets of Good judged items (`target_bins_per_line` judged bins per line, `target_tracer_runs` compared tracer runs), otherwise no data, and shows the excluded share. - Guards (CWE-501, revised after the security review of ARV-104e and its re-check): every row is read under its table's key: a manual count's line, bin, observer and revision; a tracer run's id, and its observer, tracer code and device join time (script 0048's `ux_tracer_run_join`); a line bin's zone, line, bin and version; a queue minute's zone and minute; a queue bin's and a zone health bin's zone, bin and revision. A key's time is placed by its instant alone (its ticks, as DateTime equality and `timestamptz` compare them, whatever its kind). A row whose key cannot be placed (null, outside the years 2000 to 2999, off its minute or bin, out of scope, a manual count's bin outside the planned days, as captured) is left out and counted per kind. Of the rows of one key, the highest revision decides before any value is checked, and every row of that revision is checked: when one holds a value that cannot be (a time that is not UTC; below 0 or above its bound: 10,000 crossings for a manual count, as captured; 1,000,000 people, crossings or tracks in a stored bin; a mean wait above a day; not a number; more outcomes than entrants; another bin length) or its rows disagree (copies equal in every value count once; a copy that differs only in its time's kind is equal, and refused), the key is unusable: its rows are counted, the key is listed in `LeftOutInputs.UnusableKeys` with its kind and reason, and no earlier revision stands in (a queue bin, health bin or minute counts as not stored, a line's crossings as unknown, a line and bin of manual counts is not judged). Superseded revisions are never used; those that cannot be, or disagree, are counted too. Tracer runs are placed by their ids alone and grouped before any is checked: rows of one run id that disagree, and two runs of one observer, tracer code and device join time (a conflict, never one wait counted twice), are all left out, whatever their values; a batch whose rows disagree on the observer or the offset, or hold a run that cannot be (as script 0048 checks: the tracer code pattern, UTC times in the years 2000 to 2999, a zone in scope, a corrected join on a planned day, an offset within 5 minutes, corrected times equal to device times minus the offset, more than 0 and at most 3 hours), is not used at all, as ARV-104b refuses such a batch at capture. Chosen over stopping the comparison with a problem, so the other zones keep their results. Every list is counted as it is read (CWE-400): a list's own count is never trusted. Above 1,000,000 rows of a kind, 50 zones, 200 lines, 62 windows or 3,200 bins of the windows (CWE-120), or with an inconsistent scope, nothing is compared and the result says why. ### Availability during the pilot (ARV-118, Proposed)

The pilot criterion "availability 99 percent of operating hours" (D6) as built, a product-owner proposal for the KPI annex (TC-83, docs/product/decisions.md): build it now and adjust the rule, not the ledger, if the client defines availability differently.

```
operating minute   a UTC minute from the start of the site's ledger (the first whole minute at or after
                   the first publication of its zone profile) whose site local time lies in the site's
                   operating hours, outside an announced maintenance window
available minute   every queue zone of the zone profile in force at the minute has
                   a live snapshot younger than T_stale (150 s) whose minute has reached the minute
                   and trails real time by at most T_lag (180 s), and a queue_minute row
availability       = available operating minutes / operating minutes        (none when there are no operating minutes)
availability_strict = available operating minutes / (operating + maintenance minutes)
                     (maintenance counted as unavailable rather than as not operating; none when both are 0)
```

- Operating hours (`OperatingCalendar`, Ariva.Core/Availability): weekly hours per weekday in the site's local time (its airport's IANA zone; UTC for a site without one), at most four intervals a day, an interval closing at or before its opening running into the next day (18:00 to 02:00); dated exceptions replace the intervals that open on one local day (closed, or other hours); announced maintenance windows (whole UTC minutes, at most 7 days) take operating minutes out of both sides of the ratio. A site without a calendar version in force is open around the clock.
- Recorded before it counts: a weekly version counts from its first local day when recorded (server time) before that day starts; an exception when recorded before its day starts; a maintenance window only when recorded before it starts. A window recorded at or after its start does not count at all (the API refuses it; the table's check `recorded_utc < starts_utc` refuses such a row written any other way, and the calendar ignores one should it exist). Entries change or are removed only before they take effect. So a minute's calendar state is fixed before the minute, and a recomputation gives the same answer.
- Daylight saving: the operating minutes of a local day are the UTC minutes whose local time lies in its hours, so the skipped hour has none and the repeated hour counts twice; the ledger stores minutes in UTC with their local date.
- The ledger (`availability_minute`, script 0042): one row per site and UTC minute, written once by Ariva.Api.Cronz (every minute, a per-site advisory lock, `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`), with the calendar state (Operating, Closed, Maintenance), the state (Available, Unavailable, Unobserved), the reasons (StaleZone, MissingMinute, StreamLag, NoPublishedZones, NotObservedLive) and the number of zones behind each. A minute is decided once its end is 120 s past (the grace for the stream's lateness and checkpoint) and read live from Redis until 180 s after that. The inputs are Redis snapshots and the database only.
- Start of a site's ledger (ARV-150, the owner's decision of 2026-10-10 on finding F3 of ARV-104j): a site's ledger starts at the first whole minute at or after the first publication of its zone profile (the earliest `published_on` of its published or retired versions): a profile published at 06:37:30 starts it at 06:38 (the profile in force at a minute is the one published at or before its start), one published at 06:37:00 at 06:37. The minutes before the site existed are never in the denominator. Every minute from the start counts, the minute before the sensors' first push included (MissingMinute); a later version of the profile never moves the start; a site never published has no ledger minute. The 0012 trigger refuses any change to a published or retired version and its deletion, so the first publication can only move earlier (an insert can add an earlier version), which never moves a stored start: a stored cursor is never moved. A site the job sees for the first time while its first minute is still live or ahead stores that start in a pass of its own: before its time zone is resolved, committed with nothing else, the minutes decided from the next run, so a first minute at the very edge of the live window is caught up as Unobserved; so neither a missed run, an unknown time zone nor a pass that fails later can move it. Exceptions: a site published before the job ever ran for it, or while Cronz was down (its first minute already past the live window when the job first sees it), starts at the latest decidable minute, so no history before the ledger ran is filled as unobserved (that downtime is left out; downtime after the start counts, as Unobserved or MissingMinute); a first publication stored more than 60 s ahead of the job's clock is not trusted (CWE-501): the ledger starts as if it were not there and goes on from its cursor, the minutes before the profile is in force are NoPublishedZones, none is left unwritten once the publication is trusted, and Cronz warns once per site (the site code only) so the hosts' clocks can be compared. Rule version 2 (`rule_version`) from ARV-150; version 1 started a site seen for the first time at the latest decidable minute, up to its grace before the publication (the rehearsal's 3 NoPublishedZones minutes). Rows already written keep their version and are never rewritten.
- Catch-up after downtime (deterministic): a minute decided after its live window uses the database alone. A missing queue_minute row proves it Unavailable (MissingMinute, NotObservedLive); otherwise liveness can no longer be proven and it is Unobserved (NotObservedLive), which the ratio counts as not available. Past minutes are never marked available from current Redis state.
- Plausibility of a snapshot (CWE-501): a snapshot published more than 60 s ahead of the job's clock, or describing a minute that ends more than 60 s ahead of it (`MinuteUtc + 1 min - now > 60 s`), is no evidence of liveness and counts as stale (StaleZone), never as available; the minute in progress (ending within 60 s) is accepted.
- Time zone: a site whose airport's IANA zone is not known on the Cronz host is skipped for the run (one warning per site, no minutes written, cursor unmoved), never decided in UTC; a site without an airport uses UTC.
- Reported per local day, per week (Monday to Sunday; a week cut by the range holds fewer days) and over a range of at most 92 days (`GET api/v1/sites/{siteCode}/availability`), with unobserved, maintenance and closed minutes and the reason counts apart, and both ratios: `availability` (maintenance minutes not operating) and `availabilityMaintenanceAsUnavailable` (maintenance minutes counted as operating and not available), so that pre-declared maintenance cannot silently inflate the figure. The KPI annex should either cap the maintenance minutes a site may declare per month or take the stricter ratio (TC-83).

Thresholds T_stale (the screens' staleness, TC-09), T_lag, the grace and the live window are Proposed and configurable (`Availability:*`).

## F19. Clock drift

Source: D5. Threshold is an assumption to tune; stability rule Proposed.

```
offset_s(t) = t_sensor - t_reference        reference = site NTP or PTP source
alarm when |offset_s| > 500 ms
stable when the standard deviation of offset_s over the last 10 readings < 50 ms   (Proposed)
```

Above the threshold: if stable, subtract the offset from the sensor's timestamps and flag `Degraded`; otherwise mark the sensor `Degraded` and its zone's waits across sensor boundaries unreliable.

Test: offsets 620, 610, 630 ms (stable): corrected. Offsets 100, 900, 300 ms: not stable, sensor degraded.

Implementation (ARV-023, `ClockOffset`): each push gives one reading, the device's send time minus Ariva's receive time (network delay included, a few milliseconds on a site LAN). The estimate is an exponentially weighted moving average with weight 0.2 for the newest reading; the stability test uses the last 10 raw readings and needs at least 3. The clock is Ok while the estimate and every recent reading are within 500 ms; otherwise Corrected (event times shifted by the estimate) when stable, and Unreliable when not.

```
governing bin of minute m   the stored bin [s, s + L) with s + L <= m + 1 min < s + 2L   (the bin that ended last by m's end)
ConservationResidual(m)     |r| of that bin, in people                       once r is known (both boundaries) or the bin is final
TrackCompletion(m)          100 x exited / entered of that bin, in percent    once the bin is final (or has ended with no track or every track exited)
OccupancyOutOfRange(m)      minutes of that bin outside 0..capacity           once the end boundary was sampled or the bin is final
```

- Minutes are the zone's stored queue minutes, as for the nowcast and the bin P90; a bin's value holds for the minutes from its last one until the next bin's last one. ## F22. Zone profile validity and geometry hash

ARV-016. A zone profile version is publishable when:

- every zone is a simple polygon of 3 to 200 vertices with non-zero area, every vertex inside its level (0 to width, 0 to depth metres), no two vertices closer than 1 cm, and no edge touching another that is not its neighbour, where a vertex on or closer than 1 cm to an edge that is not its own touches it (F4, segment intersection, ARV-161);
- zone names are unique in the profile, ignoring case; line names likewise;
- every queue zone has at least one entry line and exactly one exit line, each lying on one edge of that zone (both ends within 1 cm of the same edge);
- every overflow zone has an overflow entry line on one of its edges; service, staff and overflow zones hang off a queue zone on the same level; only service and staff zones name a desk;
- count lines have length (at least 1 cm) and both ends inside their level.

Coordinates are rounded to the millimetre (half away from zero, negative zero written as zero) when stored.

Geometry hash: SHA-256, lower-case hex, of the UTF-8 text below, fixed when the version is published and quoted by evidence packs and signed contracts. It contains no ids of the profile itself, so equal geometry gives an equal hash whatever the order of editing.

```
ariva-zone-profile-v1
zone|<name>|<kind>|<level id, 32 hex>|<queue zone name or empty>|<desk id, 32 hex, or empty>|<x y,x y,...>
...                                   (one line per zone, ordered by name, ordinal)
line|<name>|<role>|<zone name or empty>|<level id>|<x y>|<x y>
...                                   (one line per line, ordered by name, ordinal)
```

Numbers are written with three decimals in invariant culture. Changing this form needs a new prefix (`ariva-zone-profile-v2`) while v1 stays verifiable.

Not in the hash: a queue zone's lane category (ARV-057) and the physical capacity of a queue zone or overflow band (ARV-114a). Neither changes a count or a wait; both are part of the published version and frozen with it, and a change needs a new version like any other. F22 stays `ariva-zone-profile-v1`.

Tests: the reference profile in `ZoneProfileTests` (snake 24 x 12 m at (10, 10) with an entry and an exit line, an overflow band with its entry line, one count line) hashes to `fd3d7d1585070bd347de877d87aa3bb4e28d2b571081986650ec934471d9a1da`, recomputed independently in Python; moving a vertex by 1 mm changes the hash, by 0.4 mm does not.

### Portable geometry hash (ARV-122a, owner decision 2026-10-11)

v1 names each level and desk by its database id, so the same geometry at another site, or rebuilt in another database, never gives the same v1 hash. The owner decided on 2026-10-11 to keep v1 exactly as it is (it is the hash a published version keeps, and validation campaigns, frozen results, devices and evidence packs quote it) and to add a second hash for comparing geometry across sites: computed on request (`ZoneProfile.ComputePortableGeometryHash`), never stored. Its text is v1's with its own first line, each level named by its terminal's code and its own code, and each desk by its checkpoint's code (unique in the site) and its own code:

```
ariva-zone-profile-portable-v1
zone|<name>|<kind>|<terminal code>/<level code>|<queue zone name or empty>|<checkpoint code>/<desk code>, or empty|<x y,x y,...>
line|<name>|<role>|<zone name or empty>|<terminal code>/<level code>|<x y>|<x y>
```

Everything else (order, rounding, culture, what is left out) is v1's. Topology codes never change after creation and are unique among the live children of their parent (a checkpoint's code also across its site), and they hold no slash or bar, so the names are unambiguous within a site; a site with two live terminals of one code (possible only at two airports) is refused at export for that reason. The site configuration export answers the published profile's v1 and portable hashes, the import answers its draft's portable hash and refuses a profile that does not reproduce the one in its bundle, and the round trip between two sites compares the portable hash after the copy is published.

Tests: the same reference profile, its level named `T1/L0`, hashes to `a32ba7a122c54c0fc9a5e8deabb146b21be26383ed766dab6eb34ce5ca4fb527`, and with a service zone `Service A` (40, 10, 2 x 2 m) on desk `IMM/D01` to `fdbe688e554dfad6f291b6be56591ec2f25e79fbdcf54653dbbfc11a78876131`, both recomputed independently in Python by a script that also reproduces v1's reference hash; the same geometry at another site with other ids gives the same portable hash and a different v1 hash; an unnamed level or desk gives none (`ZoneProfileTests`, region Portable geometry hash; integration `SiteConfigurationTests`; e2e `site-configuration.spec.ts`).

## Weekly summary figures (ARV-119, Proposed)

The figures of one site's local week, computed on request from stored data (nothing new is stored), for the report tool of ARV-146 to lay out (`WeeklySummaries` in Ariva.Core/Reports, read by `WeeklySummaryReader`, served by `ISvcWeeklySummary`; no endpoint, template or document in ARV-119). Each choice below is a Proposed point of the ARV-119 entry in docs/product/decisions.md.

```
week        Monday 00:00 to the next Monday 00:00, local time of the site's airport (UTC for a site without one)
day d       DailyReports.DayBounds(Monday + d): 23 or 25 hours on a clock change; a skipped midnight starts the day at
            its first valid minute; the days follow each other without a gap
hour h      from the day's start in steps of one hour, the last one ending at the day's end (shorter after a half-hour
            change); 167 to 169 hours in a week
P_p(lane, hour)   the weighted nearest rank of the hour's minute histograms merged (F7), exact to 30 seconds
P_p(lane, day), P_p(lane, week)   the same over the day's or the week's hours merged, never averaged
peak(lane, day)   the hour with the highest P90 among hours with at least 20 waits, the earlier label on a tie
                  (the daily report's rule, so a lane's day reads as that day's report)
peak(lane, week)  the same over the week's hours, the earlier hour on a tie
peak hours        the site's 10 lane-hours with the highest P90 among hours with at least 20 waits
                  (then the earlier hour, then the zone)
overflow minutes(lane, day)   distinct minutes of the day in which any band of the lane held anyone (TC-19)
track completion  sum over final bins of min(exited, entered) / sum of entered (F18; never an average of bin rates;
                  none without a track)
bins below target final bins whose own completion is below 0.90 (F11, F18's tolerance of 1e-9)
time to acknowledge   acknowledged - raised, in minutes, over the acknowledged alerts raised in the week
uptime(device)    100 x (1 - outage minutes / elapsed minutes of the week), outages clipped to the week and to now,
                  overlapping outages once (the daily report's rule)
desk state seconds    sum over the stored desk minutes of the seconds in each state (closed, idle, serving, paused, unknown)
```

- Lanes: every queue zone of the published profile and every zone with minutes in the week, with the lane category of the newest profile that names it (as the daily report). Per lane and day: passengers, waits, P50, P90, the longest queue, the peak hour, whether any hour was provisional or stored without a histogram, overflow minutes, the bins stored and those Degraded, the alerts raised on the lane, and every local hour; per lane over the week the same totals. Hours keep their local labels and their UTC start, so the repeated hour of an autumn change is two hours with the same label.
- The local week is computed in .NET from the airport's time zone (`SiteTimeZones`, TimeZoneConverter): the hour and day bounds go to the database as parameters and every grouping uses them (`width_bucket`), so PostgreSQL's time zone data never decides a local day. A site whose airport names a zone the host does not know is refused, never summarised in UTC (as the availability ledger).
- Weeks: the date of a Monday, from the current local week (still running: figures up to the request, `complete` false, days not yet started marked) back 57 weeks (about the daily report's 400 days); any other date is refused. A week before a site's data, or partly before it, lists its days empty: no passengers, no percentile (null, never 0), no peak, and in the ledger no recorded minute and no ratio.
- Data quality (`DataQuality.View` only, with availability): the latest revision of every stored bin (`queue_bin`) and health bin (`zone_health_bin`) whose start falls in the week; bins by status and quality, the bins Degraded for their own track completion alone (ARV-114b) apart; track completion and the bins below its target over final bins; the conservation residual's size over the bins where it was measured (count, sum, largest, mean); the occupancy minutes checked against a physical capacity and those outside it. The site's figures add the lanes' counts and take the rates again from the sums.
- Alerts: those raised in the week that the reader's roles see (the alert screens' rule, as the daily report), counted by severity, rule (with its latest name) and local day. Time to acknowledge covers acknowledged alerts only, whenever acknowledged (an alert raised late on Sunday and acknowledged on Monday counts); P50 and P90 by nearest rank, mean and longest. Alerts never acknowledged are counted apart, still open or closed without an acknowledgement, never as zero minutes or as the week's end. Escalated alerts are counted.
- Availability: the ledger's minutes per local date of the week (ARV-118, ARV-150; the same query as the availability read), per day and in total, with both ratios.
- Time in each desk state: the seconds per state with the desk minutes they came from, per day and week; no utilisation figure (TC-45 is open). Per-desk rows are border desks (Immigration and Emigration checkpoints) and only for `BorderDesks.View` holders. Lane aggregates per checkpoint kind and lane go to every reader, with the desks that had open time per cell; a reader without `BorderDesks.View` gets a border lane cell by cell, each cell only where at least 3 desks had open time in it (idle, serving or paused above 0; closed desks do not count): the week when 3 desks were open in the week, the days only when every day with open time had 3 (all days or none, so the week less the days shown never gives a withheld day), the desk count only with the week. A cell of one or two open desks would be those desks' own figures; desks that only had closed or unknown minutes (the desk engine writes every desk's minute) never make up the three. The rule is a small-cell rule like the immigration screen's, which counts desks with transactions per window instead. Airport checkpoints' lanes (check-in and security) only to `AirportDesks.View` holders, never per desk (the handler's own counters wait for its module). A desk key shared by checkpoints of more than one kind, and any other checkpoint kind, are left out.
- Bounds (CWE-120): one week per request; at most 200 lanes, 30,000 desk, lane and day rows, 20,000 alerts, 2,000 devices and 20,000 outages; a read that passes one is refused (`TooLarge`), never cut short.
- Rounding: percentiles and minutes to 2 decimals (percentiles are exact to 30 seconds already), track completion to 4 decimals, desk seconds to whole seconds.

Tests: `Ariva.UnitTests/Reports/WeeklySummaryTests` (Monday 9:00 with 17 waits of 5 minutes and 10:00 with 3 of 20 give the day P90 20.5, where the average of the hours' P90s would say 13, and with Thursday's 25 waits of 3 minutes the week's P50 3.5 and P90 5.5; Dubai weeks of 168 hours, Berlin's 2026 spring and autumn weeks of 167 and 169 hours with 02:00 twice on 25 October, Beirut's 29 March starting at 01:00, Lord Howe Island's Sunday of 23.5 hours ending with a 30-minute hour; Mondays from the current week back 57 weeks; an hour of 19 waits is no peak; ten peak hours, the earlier first on a tie; empty days; track completion 0.986 from 986 of 1,000 tracks where the lanes' rates average 0.97; acknowledgements after 2, 4, 6 and 30 minutes give P50 4, P90 30, mean 10.5, with one open and one closed alert never acknowledged counted apart; uptime 98.33 and 63.33 percent on a Wednesday at noon; per-desk rows for border roles only; for an airport role a one-desk border lane withheld, three configured desks with one open withheld, a lane open on three desks over the week but on one on a day showing its week and no day, and a lane whose every day with open time had three desks showing every day; the summary round-trips through JSON) and `Ariva.IntegrationTests/Reports/WeeklySummaryTests` (the reference week built from the simulator, seed 9303: each lane's day equals that day's daily report, every hour equals the engine's own minutes merged, the neighbour site's copy of every row never counts, an airport role gets no desk row and each border cell only as the rule allows (checked against the simulator's own desk states), a desk key shared with a soft-deleted checkpoint of another kind counts nowhere, check-in and security lanes go to the duty manager as lanes only, availability equals the ledger read, reads refused at their bounds, Berlin's autumn week of 169 hours on the database).
