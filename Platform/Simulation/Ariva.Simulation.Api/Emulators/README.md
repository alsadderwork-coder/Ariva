# Emulators

The emulated partners of the demo days (ADR-0025; ARV-139b: one day per scenario site, DMO and AUH-TA). Every emulator plays the shared demo clock: the sensor emulator's (`api/v1/simulation/sensors`: start, pause, speed, jump, stop minute). Each time a demo minute completes, the sensors push it to Ingest and every feed emulator (`IDemoMinuteSink`) plays the same minute, so sensors, the AODB and AMAN describe one moment. The feeds lay the demo day on real minutes (`FeedTime`): the minute a run starts or jumps to is the current wall minute and later minutes follow one minute apart, so at speed 1 the feeds are on real time and faster runs move ahead of it. Each feed emulator plays its minutes in order on its own queue, so a slow partner never holds the sensors back; a feed more than 120 minutes behind loses its oldest queued minutes (counted as `clockMinutesDropped` in the feeds status and logged once per run). A manual `play` re-anchors the feeds when it does not follow the last minute played, so mixing it with a running clock gives the same flights on other dates.

## What each emulator sends

- **AMAN** (`Simulation:Aman`, both border sides by default): a desk session change when a scenario desk opens, pauses or closes (the first minute of a run states every desk with a session); each desk with a session reports its one-minute interval (ARV-117c, `AmanDeskProcess`): the people the scenario's lane served that minute, shared between its active desks by their speed, are worked through by each desk as discrete transactions (a family of 1, 2 or 3 people, 1.25 on average; documents are people), each needing its people times the desk's service time per person times a variation of mean 1 (0.5 plus an exponential of mean 0.5, at most 4); a transaction is counted whole in the interval in which it completes (not spread over the minutes it spans, as AMAN counts transactions processed), with its service time (the desk's working seconds on it) and its cycle time (from the previous transaction's start at the desk to its start, in open time: paused minutes do not count, idle minutes do, the contract's "start to next start while open"); the interval reports the count, documents, mean and nearest-rank P90 service time and mean cycle time of the transactions it completed, all zero when none; the first transaction of a session (or after a gap over 3,600 s) has its own service time as its cycle; a pause keeps the transaction in progress, a close drops it. So AMAN's lane cycle time follows the served people: per transaction (1.25 people) and, after a lull, with the idle time between starts (formulas F8 and F10; since ARV-117d Ariva's published desk term reads the mean service time per document instead, which leaves both out); the scenario has no walk-up gap; each e-gate in service reports attempts, accepted and rejected with rejects by coarse category, any category under 3 folded into `Other`; each inbound flight's lane demand two hours out (booked) and 30 minutes out (boarded), split by the carrier's mix, 40 % of citizens and residents e-gate eligible, transfers excluded. Codes are AMAN's own (`IN01` to `IN22`, `EGIN1` to `EGIN6`, `OUT01` to `OUT22`, `EGOUT1` to `EGOUT4`; `GET api/v1/simulation/feeds/aman-codes` lists the Ariva desk each stands for), so Ariva's AMAN desk code mappings are exercised. Records go to Kafka (`aman.feed.*.v1`, keyed by desk, gate or flight, JSON as Ariva reads it, acks from all replicas) when `Simulation:Aman:Kafka:BootstrapServers` is set (SASL credentials only with `SaslSsl`, and `Plaintext` or `SaslPlaintext` only to loopback or in-cluster brokers), and to Ariva's immigration endpoints with AMAN's integration client when it is set.
- **Mock immigration system** (`Simulation:Immigration`, departures by default): the same contracts through the generic REST endpoints with its own client and no Kafka, as a non-AMAN system integrates.
- **AODB, AUH-TA** (ARV-139b, `Simulation:Aodb:Sites:AUH-TA:Client`, or `aodbSites` in `PUT clients`): the AUH-TA scenario's arrivals at airport `AUH`, terminal `A`, pushed as AIDX to the Ariva site `AUH-TA` with a client of its own (an integration client an administrator registers for AUH-TA with `flights:write`), so a refused call on one site never drops the other site's token; without a client it keeps its ACRIS snapshot (`GET aodb/sites/AUH-TA/acris/flights`, the same API key) and pushes nothing.
- **AMAN does not play AUH-TA** (ARV-139b decision, to confirm with the owner): AUH-TA has no AMAN desk codes, its counters' states come from their staff and service zones alone (ARV-116), an AMAN session would take precedence over them, and contract V1's lanes have no GCC, DIP or TRF (ARV-130). AMAN and the mock immigration system play the reference site only.
- **AODB** (`Simulation:Aodb`): the reference scenario's arrivals and departures at the site's airport (other airport, stand, gate and aircraft fixed per flight code), each leg's estimate published an hour before and its block time when it happens. AIDX notifications to `api/v1/integration/sites/{site}/aodb/aidx`: every leg at a run's first minute, then the legs that changed. ACRIS: `GET aodb/acris/flights` with the API key Ariva's outbound endpoint presents, `Last-Modified` and 304 on `If-Modified-Since`.

REST calls go to `Simulation:Ariva:IntegrationUrl` for the Ariva site `Simulation:Ariva:SiteCode`: an immigration contract as `{ "items": [...] }` (at most 500) to `api/v1/integration/sites/{site}/immigration/{desk-sessions|desk-interval-stats|egate-interval-stats|inbound-lane-demand}` with an `Idempotency-Key` of the seed, date, minute and contract, so a replayed minute is a replay for Ariva. The clients exchange their credentials at `api/v1/auth` with a TOTP code from a newer step each time (Ariva's replay guard), keep the token until 60 seconds before it expires and send `X-TOTP-Code` on every call when their policy asks.

## Mock AMAN Integration API

For Ariva's outbound AMAN connector (`TotpClientCredentials`, ARV-050):

- `POST aman/api/v1/auth` with `{ clientId, clientSecret, totpCode }` answers `{ accessToken, expiresAt, sessionId }` (AMAN's shape, no-store). Every failure is the same 401 `{"error":"invalid_client"}`; a TOTP step is accepted once per client; `Simulation:Aman:Mock:AuthPerMinute` exchanges per address per minute (429 beyond). Clients are configured in `Simulation:Aman:Mock:Clients`: `ClientId`, `SecretSha256` (never the secret), `TotpSecret` (Base32) and `PerRequestTotp`.
- `GET aman/api/v1/feed/{contract}?after=&limit=` with the token (and a current `X-TOTP-Code` when the client's policy asks) answers what the emulated AMAN published after a sequence number, oldest first, at most 500, in AMAN's `Result` envelope `{ data: { items, next }, hasErrors, errorMessages }`. The real AMAN API's paths and paging are To confirm.

## Endpoints

Under `api/v1/simulation/feeds`, behind an operator key: `GET` (read) for each emulator's status (`aodbSites` lists the other scenario sites' AODBs); `GET aman-codes` (read); `POST play` with `minute` (control, 60 a minute per key) plays one minute on every feed at once, for tests and demos that need a given moment; `PUT clients` (control) with `aman`, `immigration` and `aodb` credentials (`clientId`, `clientSecret`, `totpSecret`, `perRequestTotp`) as Ariva issued them, and `aodbSites` with the credentials of other scenario sites' AODBs by site code (`{"aodbSites":{"AUH-TA":{...}}}`; any other key is refused without being echoed). Secrets go in and never come back out.

ACRIS: `GET aodb/acris/flights` (the reference site) and `GET aodb/sites/{site}/acris/flights` (any scenario site; 404 for another), both with the AODB's API key.

## Validation observers (ARV-104i)

The scenario knows the evening's true counts, waits and desk states; the validation emulator reports them to Ariva as validation observers would, so a validation campaign is rehearsed end to end before any site work (wiki 07 section 8 has the field engineer's runbook).

- **Truth** (`ValidationTruth`): the scenario day laid on real time from `dayStartUtc` (the instant of the scenario's 00:00; the sensor emulator lays it the same way at speed 1, and its last such instant is `clockDayStartUtc` in the status). People are the sensor emulator's whole people: person n of a queue enters when the cumulative arrivals reach n and leaves when the cumulative departures reach n, at the same fraction of the minute, so a line's true count is what the sensors push and a minute's true wait is the mean of its entrants' waits as Ariva's queue engine pairs them (an exit stamped before its entry has no wait, as Ariva rejects such a pair). A desk's state is what an observer at the desk sees: closed and out of service are Closed, and a desk the scenario hides from the sensors is seen as it is (Paused when it pauses, otherwise Serving while people queue, Idle when none do).
- **What the observers report** (`RehearsalPlanner`): a campaign's lines, zones and desks are matched to the scenario by the demo profiles' names (zone `A-VIS` is the queue A-VIS, its `Entry` and `Exit` lines count its people in and out, desk `AR-08` is the server AR-08); an overflow or count line, or a zone or desk the scenario does not have, is counted as without truth and never guessed. A count per line in scope and 15-minute UTC bin wholly inside the window; one tracer per queue zone every `tracerEveryMinutes` (10 by default), joining at the middle of the first minute of its slot whose entrants have a wait and waiting their mean wait (what Ariva compares a tracer with, formulas F18), labelled T-01 upwards (never a name); per bin, one desk batch of at most 20 desks per observer, each minute's state. Lines, tracers and desks go to the observers in turn (desks only to observers Ariva shows desks to).
- **Errors** (`ObserverErrors`, all systematic so the outcome is known in advance): `countErrorPercent` (-50 to 50) on every line or on the lines named in `countErrorLines`, rounded half away from zero; `tracerErrorMinutes` (-30 to 30) and `tracerErrorPercent` (-50 to 50) on each tracer's wait, which stays between 1 second and 3 hours; slips: `missedBinsPercent` of count bins and desk batches not sent, `missedMinutesPercent` of desk minutes left unobserved, drawn from `seed` so the same seed misses the same items.
- **Sending** (`ArivaObserverClient`, `ValidationEmulator`): each observer signs in to `Simulation:Ariva:MainUrl` through `api/auth/login` with its user name, password and, for an account with an authenticator, a TOTP code of a step it has not used (Ariva's replay guard), keeps the token in memory until a minute before it expires (no refresh cookie is kept: cookies are off), reads the running campaigns of the site as Ariva shows them to it, and sends counts, tracer batches (the simulator's clock as the device clock, read at every send) and desk batches through the capture API, each with an `Idempotency-Key` of the campaign, the account and the item, so a rehearsal sent twice stores nothing twice. A call without an answer, 408, 429 or 5xx is sent once more after a second with the same key. Sign-ins are bounded (CWE-287): besides its first, an observer signs in at most once more per rehearsal (a token about to expire, or a 401 to a token issued earlier); when that sign-in fails, when it would need a third, or when Ariva answers 401 to a token it has just issued, its token is dropped and it is taken out (`TakenOut`): that call counts as failed and its remaining items as not sent, so a disabled or reset account is never pushed into lockout and never holds Ariva's sign-in limit for the simulator's address. TOTP steps are kept per account by the user name as Ariva matches it (trimmed, NFKC, lower case). Ariva decides everything: an account without the permission, a campaign's creator or starter, a campaign not running, a bin outside the planned days are Ariva's refusals, counted in the report with Ariva's own title. One rehearsal at a time, `Simulation:Validation:RehearsalsPerMinute` per operator key (10), at most 240 minutes and 2,000 calls, stopped after `RehearsalTimeoutSeconds` (180).
- **Accounts**: `Simulation:Validation:Observers` (the simulation appsettings secret) or `PUT api/v1/simulation/validation/observers`: Ariva accounts an administrator created with the Validation observer role and the site only. User names, passwords and seeds go in and never come back out, and are never logged; reports and the status name observers by number.
- **Ariva's address**: `Simulation:Ariva:MainUrl`, the Ariva.Api.Main of the simulator's own deployment only: a loopback address, or `api-main-service` alone or with the pod's own namespace (`K8S_NAMESPACE_NAME` from the downward API, which the chart sets) and `.svc` or `.svc.cluster.local`; any other name, namespace or address is refused at start (`ArivaTargetSettings.IsOwnDeploymentMain`). The client follows no redirect, keeps no cookie and uses no proxy.
- **Logs**: like Ariva's hosts, the simulator writes its log lines as JSON to `LogFile:Path` when it is set (`SimulationLogFile`), so the E2E run's log scan reads them too.

Under `api/v1/simulation/validation`, behind an operator key: `GET` (read) the status (Ariva's address configured or not, the observers by number with whether each has a second factor and holds a token, whether a rehearsal runs, `clockDayStartUtc`, the last report); `GET truth?site=&dayStartUtc=&fromMinute=&toMinute=&queues=` (read; `Simulation:Validation:TruthReadsPerMinute` per key, 60, then 429 with Retry-After) the truth over at most 360 minutes: per queue and minute the people in and out and the entrants' mean wait, each server's observed state per minute, the sensor outages laid on real time (DMO: S-17 over A-VIS, 18:20 to 18:30); `PUT observers` (control); `POST rehearsals` (control) with `siteCode`, `campaignId`, `scenarioSite` (DMO by default), `scenarioSeed` (optional: 409 when the day runs another), `dayStartUtc`, `fromMinute`, `toMinute`, the errors above, `tracerEveryMinutes` and `seed`; it answers the report (what was planned, recorded, replayed, refused, failed or not sent per kind, the tracer runs and desk minutes sent, the observers' states, the first refusals), or 409 when it cannot start (another runs, no Ariva address, no observer, another seed) and 429 beyond the key's rehearsals of the minute. Bodies of these endpoints are at most 16 KB; values that fail a check are never echoed.
